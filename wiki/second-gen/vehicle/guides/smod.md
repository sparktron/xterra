# SMOD: radiator cooler failure, coolant in the transmission

[Guides](index.md) · [Reference home](../index.md) · Fix: [Radiator replacement and bypass](radiator-smod-fix.md) · Related: [ATF change and flush](atf-change.md), [Cooling upgrades](cooling-upgrades.md)

"SMOD" is owners' shorthand for "strawberry milkshake of death". The automatic transmission's fluid cooler runs through the bottom tank of the radiator. If that cooler tube cracks inside the radiator, coolant and ATF mix into a pink froth that ruins the transmission [S1][S3].

| | |
|---|---|
| Affects | 2005-2010 Xterra, Frontier and Pathfinder with the automatic transmission. Nissan's own bulletin covers all of them [S1]. The bypass write-up notes it doesn't apply to manual-transmission trucks [S2] |
| Cause | "An internal crack on the oil cooler tube" in the factory (Calsonic) radiator, per Nissan [S1][S3] |
| Risk | Nissan called it "a small percentage of vehicles" [S1]. A transmission rebuilder reported that about half of the RE5R05A cores they received had coolant intrusion from failed radiator coolers [S6] |

## Symptoms

- **Pink, milky or frothy fluid** on the transmission dipstick, in the radiator, or in the coolant reservoir [S3].
- Transmission trouble first: hard shifts, slipping, failure to shift, or a rumbling while driving [S3].
- Transmission shops say vehicles with coolant contamination often arrive with a torque converter clutch slip or shudder complaint [S8].

## Check yours

1. With the engine **cold** and the system not under pressure, look in the coolant reservoir and radiator, and pull the ATF dipstick. Any pink milkiness is SMOD [S3].
2. **Check the radiator's part number**, on top of the radiator. Owners have mapped the factory part numbers [S3]:

| Radiator part number | What owners report | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 21410-EA200 (early 2005) | associated with SMOD | [S3] |
| 21410-EA205 (2005-2009) | associated with SMOD | [S3] |
| 21460-EA215 (2005-2009) | associated with SMOD | [S3] |
| 21460-EA265 | associated with SMOD | [S3] |
| 21460-ZL11A (2009-2010) | isolated reports | [S3] |
| 21460-ZL31A | no cases found | [S3] |
| 21460-9CA0E (2011-2015; the source writes "9CAOE") | no known cases | [S3] |

   These are owners' compilations, not Nissan's list [S3]. Nissan's "Value Advantage" replacement radiators (for example B1D60-EA21JNW) were not affected, per one owner; only the factory Calsonic units were [S5]. If the label is gone, treat a 2005-2010 original radiator as at risk [S5].
3. **Buying a used 2005-2010 automatic?** Fresh ATF and fresh coolant on an old truck can mean someone has hidden a recent SMOD [S5].

## If you find SMOD

- **Stop driving it** [S3].
- Replace the radiator and flush **all** of the ATF. A pan drain and fill replaces only a few quarts, and the old fluid re-contaminates the new [S3]. See [Radiator replacement and bypass](radiator-smod-fix.md) and [ATF change and flush](atf-change.md).
- If the transmission is already damaged, it may need a rebuild or replacement. A rebuilder warns that a defective radiator will keep contaminating a rebuilt unit, so replace the radiator as well [S8].

## Prevention: replace the radiator or bypass its cooler

- **Replace the radiator** with one that has no history of the problem. Owners call this the best option [S3][S10].
- **Bypass the radiator's cooler** by swapping two hoses. The ATF then runs only through the separate air-to-oil cooler in front of the A/C condenser [S4][S11]. It costs nothing and takes under 30 minutes [S3]. The procedure is in [Radiator replacement and bypass](radiator-smod-fix.md#bypass-the-radiators-transmission-cooler).

**The bypass is a trade-off.** Read these before you decide:

- An owner who measured ATF temperatures found that the radiator cooler never heats the ATF: it is always cooling. Bypassing it removes a large part of the cooling capacity. Light driving may be fine on the air cooler alone, but moderately heavy load can overheat the transmission [S11].
- The same owner found that the air cooler does little below about 20-25 mph, and that his stock-cooled truck overheated into limp mode climbing mountain passes in 4LO in Drive. He recommends a transmission temperature gauge, using a lower gear on long climbs, and an auxiliary cooler or fan if you bypass [S11].
- One owner reports 6.5 years on the bypass with an added auxiliary cooler, including desert heat and a 5,500 lb trailer, without problems [S2].
- Two transmission-trade sources warn about changing the cooler circuit. One, quoted by an owner, says the torque converter lock-up piston has no return spring and relies on restrictions in the OEM cooler circuit, so a different circuit could stop the lock-up clutch from releasing [S5]. The other, which we read directly, says aftermarket radiators or auxiliary coolers can cause converter clutch apply and release problems through changes in back pressure [S8]. If you bypass, watch for lock-up shudder or harsh lock-up.

## Nissan's warranty extension (now expired)

In October 2012 Nissan extended the radiator coverage on all 2005-2010 automatic Frontier, Pathfinder and Xterra trucks. The bulletin is on NHTSA's site [S1]:

- **Up to 8 years / 80,000 mi:** no co-pay [S1]
- **8-9 years / 80,000-90,000 mi:** $2,500 co-pay [S1]
- **9-10 years / 90,000-100,000 mi:** $3,000 co-pay [S1]
- It covered damage to other components, **including the transmission**, caused by the leak, and reimbursed owners who had already paid for qualifying repairs [S1]
- Dealers see it in SERVICE COMM as warranty information bulletin WBI12-028 [S1]

The coverage ended at 10 years from the original in-service date, so it has run out on every affected truck [Unverified].
