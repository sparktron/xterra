| Shackle nuts (upper and lower) | 106 N·m (78 ft-lb) | 105 N·m (77 ft-lb) | [S1][S2] |

The front eye torque changed a lot between manuals [S1][S2]. Use your year's value. Owners quote the older 84 ft-lb [S5].

**Final-tighten every bushing bolt with the truck on its wheels**, unladen and at ride height. Nissan's "unladen" means full fuel, coolant and oil, with the spare, jack and tools in place [S1][S5].

## Removing and refitting a leaf spring (Nissan's procedure)

1. Remove the spare tire. For the right side, remove the tailpipe [S1].
2. Jack under the rear axle **just enough to take the weight**, with no compression in the leaf spring [S1].
3. Remove the four U-bolt nuts and the spring pad [S1].
4. Remove the shackle and its bushings, then the front eye nut and bolt, and lower the spring [S1].
5. Inspect the leaves, shackle, U-bolts, bump stop, pad and bushings for cracks and wear [S1].
6. Wet the rubber bushings with **soapy water**, and grease the front eye bolt and nut [S1].
7. Fit the shackle and front eye bolts **finger-tight** [S1].
8. Fit the U-bolts over the axle tube and the pad and nuts under the spring. Grease the U-bolts and nuts [S1].
9. Tighten the U-bolt nuts **diagonally and evenly** until the exposed threads under the pad are within 3 mm (0.12 in) of each other [S1].
10. Lower the axle, bounce the rear to settle it, refit the tailpipe and spare, then torque the shackle, front eye and shock nuts with the truck on level ground [S1].

**Tips from owners:**

- Do **one side at a time** [S4].
- U-bolts shouldn't be reused [S4]. Nissan's diagram marks the U-bolt nuts as replace-every-time [S1].
- To free a stuck shackle bolt, jack the axle until the body just starts to lift, then lower it slowly while tapping the bolt until it slides out [S3].
- On the **driver's side**, a black plastic fuel-system box behind the upper shackle bolt blocks access [S3].
- Disconnecting the rear ABS wires from their clips keeps them from being stretched with the axle hanging [S3].

## Lift shackles and add-a-leafs

- **Lift shackles** (Automotive Customizer, from 4x4parts.com): about 2 in of rear lift in about 3 hours. The kit sleeve goes into the factory upper bushing, so the stock bushings can usually be reused. The upper bolts have nylon-insert nuts; the lower use lock washers [S3].
- **Add-a-leaf** (Calmini, for example): pry open the pack's clamps, clamp the pack each side of the center bolt, replace the center bolt with the kit's longer one, and fit the new leaves and wedge in order. The fat end of the wedge goes to the rear. Refit the clamps, cut the excess center bolt, and add the bump-stop extensions [S4].
  - Hardest part: getting the center pin into its slot in the U-bolt plate. One owner fitted the U-bolts loosely first, then the eye bolts [S4].
  - Tighten U-bolts in rotation: as one reaches torque, the others drop off it [S4].

## Squeaks, pops and a harsh ride

- **Dry-lube the leaves.** Clean the pack with the axle hanging so the leaves part, blow it dry, then spray a **dry graphite lube** (not grease) between the leaves. Concentrate on the **curled ends** of the leaves, and keep lube off the brakes. Drive, then relube with the weight off and leave it overnight [S6].
- **Rebuild with leaf pads.** One owner's tired OME packs were transformed by new Dorman 924070 leaf pads at the leaf tips and thin steel plates between the leaves, with holes drilled for the center pin. Rubber Nissan shackle bushings (55047-EB301) replaced the polyurethane ones at the shackle end [S5].
- **Shackle slap** can be quieted by filling the gap at the shackle mount; one owner used wide zip ties [S7].
