# Spark plug replacement (VQ40DE), including the upper intake plenum

[Guides](index.md) · [Reference home](../index.md) · [Part numbers](../parts.md)

| | |
|---|---|
| Applies to | 2005-2015 Xterra, 4.0 L VQ40DE. The same job applies to the Frontier and Pathfinder with this engine [S4] |
| Difficulty | Driver's side is easy. Passenger side is moderate, because the upper intake plenum (Nissan's "intake manifold collector") has to come off [S2][S4] |
| Time | 1-3 hours [S4] |
| Interval | Replace every 105,000 mi (168,000 km). Nissan says not to check or re-gap the plugs between changes [S1][S2] |

## Tools

- 16 mm (5/8 in) spark plug socket, ideally with a magnet or rubber insert to hold the plug [S2][S4][S5]
- 3/8 in ratchet and about 9-12 in of extensions; the plugs sit very deep [S4][S5]
- 10 mm and 12 mm sockets, 10 mm deep socket, 10 mm and 12 mm wrenches. A 12 mm ratcheting wrench helps on the plenum support bolts [S4][S5]
- Torque wrench that reads in inch-pounds for the coils and plenum. One owner snapped and stripped bolts with a cheap one [S4]
- Needle-nose pliers for hose clamps, flat screwdriver, dielectric grease [S4][S5]

## Driver's side (LH bank): coils come straight out

1. Disconnect the battery negative terminal [S4].
2. Remove the engine cover. Two 10 mm bolts are at the front, and the cover pries up off two push fasteners [S5].
3. Move the harness, harness bracket and hoses above the coils aside. Disconnect each coil's connector [S2].
4. Remove each coil's 10 mm bolt and pull the coil [S2][S4].
5. Unscrew the plug with the 16 mm socket and extensions. Don't drop or knock the new plugs [S2].
6. Thread each new plug in **by hand** with the socket and extension, then torque it to 24.5 N·m (18 ft-lb) [S2][S4].
7. Push the coil on until the boot seats, then torque its bolt to 7.0 N·m (62 in-lb) [S2][S4].

## Passenger side (RH bank): plenum off first

Nissan's manual removes the throttle body, which means partly draining the coolant first [S2]. Most owners skip that. They leave the coolant hose on the bottom of the throttle body and swing the plenum aside with the throttle body still attached [S4][S5]. **Do not pull that lower coolant hose unless you have drained the coolant** [S4][S5].

1. Remove the engine cover (step 2 above) and the intake duct from the throttle body [S2][S4].
2. Disconnect from the plenum, counting each one so you can check them off at reassembly [S4][S5]:
   - the throttle body connector, and the hose that runs up and over the throttle body [S4][S5]
   - the brake booster vacuum hose and the PCV hose [S2][S5]
   - the hose that runs around the back of the plenum to the intake tube [S5]
   - the grey connector at the back near the firewall, and the harness conduit's push clips (or unbolt its bracket) [S4][S5]
3. Remove the EVAP purge solenoid and the VIAS solenoid and vacuum tank from the top. One owner found a 12 mm bolt and a 10 mm bracket bolt [S2][S4].
4. Remove the two 12 mm plenum support bolts under and behind the throttle body. They can be tight [S2][S4][S5].
5. Loosen the plenum's 5 bolts and 2 stud nuts in the reverse of the tightening sequence, then remove them [S2][S4][S5].
6. Lift the plenum up and toward the front passenger corner, off the studs, and rest it over the radiator. Mind the coolant hose still on the throttle body [S4][S5].
7. **Cover the intake ports** so nothing falls into the engine [S2][S4].
8. Pull the three old plenum gaskets out by their tabs. Fit new ones dry if the maker says so; Fel-Pro says not to add anything to theirs [S4].
9. Replace the passenger-side plugs exactly as in the driver's-side steps 3-7 [S2][S4].
10. Refit the plenum and tighten its nuts and bolts in the numbered sequence to 11.0 N·m (8 ft-lb). Don't over-tighten and crack it [S2][S4].
11. Refit the support bolts to 20.1 N·m (15 ft-lb), then the solenoids, hoses and connectors [S2].
12. Reconnect the battery. If you unplugged the throttle body, Nissan's manual calls for "Throttle Valve Closed Position Learning" [S2]. Some dash lights, such as VDC, can stay on until a short drive [S4].

## Tips

- The **cylinder 6 area at the back of the passenger side** is the tightest spot. Some owners pull one coil at a time and label the coil wires so they don't mix them up [S4].
- A light film of dielectric grease inside the coil boot, about a grain of rice, is common practice. Keep it off the electrode [S4].
- Owners disagree about lubricating the plug threads (engine oil, anti-seize, or nothing as from the factory). Lubricated threads tighten further at the same torque, so if you lubricate them, don't exceed the spec [Unverified].
- With the plenum off, owners also clean the oil residue out of it [S4].
- The PCV valve is a cheap part worth changing while you're in there, though it doesn't need the plenum off. It sits at the back of the top of the engine, at the end of the black hose from the plenum. Pull the hose (the spring clamp at the valve end is the hard part) and unscrew the plastic valve with a 24 mm (15/16 in) socket on a stubby U-joint ratchet. Fitting a small screw clamp in place of the spring clamp makes the next time easier. One owner found his valve stuck at 45,000 and 80,000 mi [S6]. Nissan's torque for the PCV valve is only 2.5 N·m (22 in-lb) [S8].
