# Driveline and wheel-end noises and vibrations

[Guides](index.md) · [Reference home](../index.md) · Related: [Engine noises](engine-noises.md), [Wheel bearings](wheel-bearing-service.md), [CV axles](cv-axles.md), [Differential fluid](differential-fluid.md), [Leaf springs](leaf-springs.md), [Lift kits](lift-kits.md)

## Which one is it?

| Symptom | Likely causes | Where to look | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vibration at **one speed range only** (for example 25-40 mph) after a lift, felt in the seat, gone in neutral | Rear pinion angle changed, a U-joint worn to the old angle, or rear springs too stiff for the load | [Vibration after a lift](#vibration-after-a-lift) | [S1] |
| Vibration at **high speed** | Driveshaft runout first, then tire balance | [Checking the driveshafts](#checking-the-driveshafts), [Tires and wheels](tires-wheels.md) | [S2] |
| Rattle from under the middle of the truck, loose rear driveshaft | Worn CV joint at the rear driveshaft's differential end | [Rear driveshaft CV joint](#rear-driveshaft-cv-joint) | [S3] |
| Clunk from the rear when you lift off and get back on the gas at about 40-60 mph (automatic) | Normal driveline slack plus the torque converter locking and unlocking, or a worn joint | [Slack or a bad joint?](#slack-or-a-bad-joint) | [S4] |
| Faint rear whine at 50-70 mph that comes and goes with throttle | Rear differential pinion bearing or a leaking pinion seal; often harmless for years | [Rear differential whine](#rear-differential-whine) | [S5] |
| Low warble from the rear under load (not coasting) after a differential swap | Gear oil too low, often from filling with the truck not level | [Rear differential whine](#rear-differential-whine) | [S6] |
| Front clunk when the wheels drop off a curb or pothole, after a lift | The upper control arm hitting the coil bucket | [Clunk after a front lift](#clunk-after-a-front-lift) | [S9] |
| Noise from a front wheel that gets louder in tight turns, or play when you rock the tire | Front wheel bearing (hub) | [Wheel bearings](wheel-bearing-service.md) | [S8] |
| High-pitched whine on acceleration that a new front wheel bearing didn't cure | The drive belt and pulleys: one owner's whine nearly went away with a new belt | [Serpentine belt](serpentine-belt.md) | [S7] |

## Checking the driveshafts

Nissan's inspection, from the 2012 manual [S2]:

1. Look for dents or cracks in the tube; a damaged shaft is replaced as an assembly [S2].
2. **For vibration at high speed, check runout first.** Turn the differential's companion flange by hand and measure the tube's runout at several points with a dial indicator [S2].
3. If runout is over the limit, unbolt the shaft at the differential flange, turn the flange 90°, 180° or 270°, refit and measure again. Still over the limit: replace the shaft [S2].
4. Hold one yoke and check each U-joint for **axial play**. Over the limit: repair or replace the joint [S2].

| Shaft | Runout limit | U-joint journal axial play | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Front, 2F1310 (4WD) | 0.6 mm (0.024 in) | 0.02 mm (0.0008 in) or less | [S2] |
| Rear, 2S1330-BJ100 steel tube (4WD) | 0.60 mm (0.0236 in) or less | 0.02 mm (0.0008 in) or less | [S2] |
| Rear, 2S1330 aluminum tube (2WD) | 1.02 mm (0.0402 in) or less | 0.02 mm (0.0008 in) or less | [S2] |

**When removing a shaft:** paint matching marks on the shaft flanges and the companion flanges first. Nissan says don't scratch them, and **don't reuse the flange bolts and nuts** [S2].

## Vibration after a lift

A lift via the rear leaf springs tilts the differential pinion. Owners' experience in one long thread [S1]:

1. **Confirm it's driveline.** One owner's vibration at 30 mph disappeared when he shifted to neutral at that speed [S1].
2. **Measure the angles on level ground,** with the truck on its wheels, not on jack stands. The transfer case output and the pinion angles should be parallel, for example -3° at the transfer case and +3° at the pinion. The driveshaft's own angle matters less [S1].
3. **Shims:** the usual rule of thumb is 1° per inch of lift. Fit the wedge between the spring and the axle perch, **thick end toward the front**, which tilts the pinion up [S1]:
   1. Support the frame on stands so the axle hangs [S1].
   2. Remove the U-bolts and fit the shim [S1].
   3. Fit new U-bolts and torque them to spec. Nissan's U-bolt torque is on the [leaf springs guide](leaf-springs.md) [S1].
   
   Owners report stock U-bolts stretch, so don't reuse them [S1].
4. **Try a U-joint.** One owner on 4° shims kept a 30-40 mph vibration until the rear U-joint was replaced. One theory: an old joint wears to its original angle and vibrates at the new one. Another owner did his in about 90 minutes for under $50 including new bolts [S1].
5. **Too much spring:** the original poster ended up nearly curing his vibration by loading about 450 lb into the cargo area. His heavy-duty rear spring packs were too stiff for the empty truck, so he took them to a spring shop to remove leaves [S1].

## Slack or a bad joint?

For a clunk on throttle changes, one owner's checks [S4]:

1. **Watch the tachometer.** At about 50 mph, lift off and get back on the gas. If the rpm jumps above where it was, the transmission downshifted or the converter unlocked. If it returns to the same rpm, look for driveline slack [S4].
   - One owner's clunk went away completely with overdrive turned off [S4].
2. **Check the rear shaft for play:** [S4]
   1. Engine off, in neutral, parking brake set hard [S4].
   2. Grip the shaft and the rear differential yoke and twist them in opposite directions; movement points to the rear CV joint [S4].
   3. Do the same at the transfer case end; movement there points to the U-joint [S4].
3. **Take the load off the shaft first.** Another owner advised chocking the front wheels, raising the rear wheels and putting it in neutral. Checked in Park on the ground, a shaft can feel tight when it isn't [S1].
4. A small clunk on an automatic coasting in top gear can be normal. Owners found it on other Nissans too [S4].

## Rear driveshaft CV joint

The 4WD rear shaft has a U-joint at the transfer case and a CV joint at the differential. One owner's very loose CV made a bad rattle. He replaced it with a $120 joint from RockAuto, and suggests doing the transfer case U-joint at the same time [S3].

1. Remove the driveshaft. Pry the cover off the back of the CV joint with a pick or small screwdriver [S3].
2. Cut off the boot clamp; the kit has a new boot [S3].
3. Remove the snap ring and tap the CV off the shaft's splines [S3].
4. **Pack the new joint with the supplied grease**, forcing it through the inside of the ball cage until it comes out the other side, like packing a wheel bearing. Turn it and repeat. Keep some grease back to fill it before the cap goes on [S3].
5. Tap the new joint onto the splines. A 27 mm socket fits around the shaft. It's fully home when the snap ring seats [S3].
6. Fit the snap ring, the cap with its O-ring, the boot and the new clamp [S3].

## Rear differential whine

- A faint whine at 50-70 mph that goes away on and off the throttle is often blamed on the pinion bearing. Some owners ran one for another 60,000-200,000 miles; others rebuilt or replaced the axle [S5].
- One owner's whine, with a slight pinion seal leak, stopped after a dealer replaced the pinion seal. The dealer called it a threading issue [S5].
- **After fitting a differential or a new cover, check the oil level with the axle level.** One owner's swapped M226 warbled under load until he leveled the truck and added about 1/3 quart more. His aftermarket cover's fill plug sat about 3/4 in lower than another owner's [S6].
- Check pinion play: parking brake on, in neutral, then feel for up-down or in-out movement at the yoke [S6].

## Clunk after a front lift

With a front spacer or coilover lift on stock upper control arms, the arm can hit the coil spring bucket at full droop. One owner found a cut about 1/32 in deep where it hit [S9]. Owners also say it can crack the arms or buckets [S9].

Options, cheapest first:
1. Go slower over bumps [S9].
2. Grind down the small lip on the bucket [S9].
3. Fit skinny bump stops. They deaden the noise but slightly reduce travel [S9].
4. Fit aftermarket upper arms that clear the bucket. The bucket edge may still need grinding [S9].

Also check sway bar links bottoming at full droop, and the lower shock bolts [S9].
