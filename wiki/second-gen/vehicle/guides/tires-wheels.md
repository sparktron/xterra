## What fits

Owners' reports from the fitment threads. Different tires of the same size fit differently: one owner found blocky, square-shouldered BFG All-Terrain T/A KOs rubbed a lot at full lock where KM2s barely touched [S3].

| Tire size (approx. diameter) | Lift | What it took | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 265/75R16 (32 in) | None | Fits a stock truck with no changes. It's the original Off-Road and PRO-4X size ([trims](../trims.md#original-wheels-and-tires)) | [S2] |
| 285/75R16 or 285/70R17 (33 in) | None | Usually the **melt mod** only, on stock wheels. One owner ran 285/70R17s on a 2008 at stock height, with full travel and full lock and no rubbing after the melt mod. Some tires need no melt at all | [S3] |
| 285/75R16 (33 in) on aftermarket wheels with less backspacing | 2-2.5 in | Trimming 0.5-1.5 in of the lower front fender. One owner also needed a 2 in body lift to stop contact in the wheel wells | [S3] |
| 285/70R17 on 17 x 8 wheels with 4.5 in backspacing | 1.5 in | Melt mod front and rear, slight fender bending behind the plastic, and cam bolts set to move the tire forward | [S2] |
| 305/70R16 (a wide 33 in) | 2.5 in | Expect fender trimming. The width, not the diameter, is what rubs | [S3] |
| 315/75R16 or 315/70R17 (35 in) | Titan swap or 2 in suspension lift, plus a 2 in body lift | Front fenders cut 1-2.5 in, lots of melt mod. Gears of 4.10 at least, and 4.56 recommended with a manual | [S2][S6] |

- **Ride height:** a bigger tire raises the truck by only half the change in diameter. A 33 in tire sticks out about 0.5 in more than a 32 in tire [S3].
- **The spare:** a 33 in (285) tire fits in the spare position, but it's tight [S3].
- **37 in tires:** owners on the forum advise against them. Parts start breaking [S6].
- **Gearing:** see [Regearing](regearing.md). One owner on 35s with stock 3.69 gears found 6th gear useless on the road [S6].

## The melt mod

Behind each front tire, the plastic wheel-well liner bulges out where a 33 in tire touches it when the wheel is turned. The melt mod reshapes that bulge [S3][S4].

1. Find the bulge on the liner just behind the front tire [S4].
2. Heat the inside corner of the bulge until the plastic is soft. A heat gun is fastest; a hair dryer works more slowly. One owner used a hair dryer, then a small lighter [S4].
   - **Don't overheat it.** A heat gun can melt the plastic right through [S4].
3. Push the softened plastic inward and wedge a short piece of 2x4 between the liner and the tire to hold it. A 2x4 worked better than a hammer [S4].
4. Work along the bottom edge, then higher up the bulge, heating and pushing [S4].
5. Spray each area with ice water right after shaping, to set it faster [S4].
6. Check the rear wheel wells for clearance too [S4].

Two months later, the writer's reshaped liner was still holding its shape [S4].

## Wheel spacers and Titan wheels

- **Hub-centric versus lug-centric:** a hub-centric spacer seats on the hub, so the hub carries the load, as the stock wheel does. A lug-centric spacer leaves the load on the studs. A "hub/wheel-centric" spacer also has a lip that centers the wheel; the writer knew of only Bora and Spidertrax making them [S5].
- **Front versus rear:** a spacer reduces backspacing. On the independent front that swings the tire through a wider arc and into more things. On the solid rear axle it adds tire clearance. One owner runs spacers on the rear only [S5].
- **Matching a Titan swap:** a Titan front end makes the front track about 3 in wider per side, so most Titan-swapped owners run 2 in spacers on the rear to match [S6].
- **Install:** torque the spacer's own nuts to spec and use red threadlocker on them. Cheap spacers with poor studs have snapped studs [S5].
- **Rear fender lip:** with spacers, plan on rolling the inner lip at the bottom rear of the rear wheel well, about 0.5 in on one truck [S5].
- **Titan wheels:** they need 6 x 4.5 to 6 x 5.5 adapters, from 1.25 to 1.5 in thick, and longer studs. Expect to rework the front liner beyond the melt mod [S2][S8][S9].

## After new tires

- **TPMS:** the Xterra registers each wheel's sensor in the order front left, front right, rear right, rear left. Nissan registers sensors after rotating tires and after replacing a sensor [S1].
  - New sensors arrive "asleep" and need an activation tool [S1].
  - Nissan's procedure without that tool needs its scan tool. Set the tires to 250/230/210/190 kPa (36/33/30/27 psi) in registration order, then drive at 40 km/h (25 MPH) or more for a few minutes [S1].
  - Torque a sensor's valve stem nut to 7.7 N·m (68 in-lb), and never use silicone lubricant on the bead [S1].
- **Rotation:** Nissan leaves the spare out of the rotation [S1].
- **Airing down** lights the low-pressure lamp below 28 psi [S1]. One owner on LT285/75R16 tires runs 32-35 psi on the road and 15 psi on trails, and has gone as low as 13 psi without unseating a bead [S2].

## What owners said about specific tires

- The original Hankook Dynapro ATMs on one 2015 were good for 15,000-20,000 miles, then got loud and wore oddly [S2].
- Goodyear DuraTracs: good on the road and in snow, but one owner trashed 3 in 2 trips on rocky trails after a Titan swap because of weak sidewalls [S10].
- Nitto Trail Grappler 315/75R16: one owner's favorite trail tire. It was quiet and good in snow for a mud tire, and needed 4 oz at most to balance [S2].
