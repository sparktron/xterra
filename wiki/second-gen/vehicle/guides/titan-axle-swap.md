# Titan swap: front suspension, M205 front differential, brakes and rear axle

[Guides](index.md) · [Reference home](../index.md) · Related: [Regearing](regearing.md), [CV axles](cv-axles.md), [Lift kits](lift-kits.md), [Tires and wheels](tires-wheels.md), [Ball joints and tie rods](ball-joints-tie-rods.md)

A "Titan swap" (TS) replaces the Xterra's front suspension with the longer Nissan Titan geometry: Titan upper and lower control arms, Titan-length coilovers, longer tie rods, and a way to reach the wider hubs. It bolts on [S1].

| | |
|---|---|
| What you gain | More wheel travel, about 9.5 in at the front by one vendor's figure; about 3-4 in of lift; and a stronger front differential if you fit the Titan M205 [S1] |
| Track width | The front gets about **3 in wider each side** (6 in total). With stock wheels the front tires stick out past the fenders, which is technically illegal on the road in some places [S1] |
| Rear to match | Most owners fit 2 in wheel spacers at the rear to match the front track, and lift the rear springs to match the height [S3][S7] |
| Ride | Owners call the ride improvement "night and day" [S1] |

## Two ways to drive the front wheels (4WD)

1. **Titan M205 front differential with Titan CV half-shafts.** This is the usual choice. One owner found a new M205 cheap enough from a parts broker that he chose it over extended axles; another said it leaves more upgrade options later [S1][S3][S8].
2. **Keep the R180 with extended half-shafts.** One registry entry used PRG extended half axles [S3].

A different, smaller swap keeps the stock suspension and track: fit only the M205 with V8 Pathfinder half-shafts. Those shafts are expensive, and you don't get the Titan arms' extra travel [S6]. A 2WD truck needs neither a differential nor CVs [S4].

## Front parts list

These are from owners' lists. Confirm part numbers with a Nissan parts counter for your VIN before ordering.

- **Control arms:**
  - Titan upper arms; lifted trucks use aftermarket ones (PRG, SPC, Rough Country 83401A, Dirt King, Z1) [S2][S3][S4].
  - Titan lower arms: OE Nissan 54500-ZR00A (RH) and 54501-ZR00A (LH), or Mevotech CMS30116 / CMS30117 [S2][S3][S4].
- **Coilovers:** Titan-length coilovers, commonly Radflo or Sway-Away 2.0-2.5 in with 600-650 lb springs [S2][S3].
- **Tie rods:** Titan inner and outer tie rods, or PRG tie-rod extensions. Owners used:
  - Hitachi TRE0003 (left outer), TRE0004 (right outer) and TRI0003 (inner) [S2];
  - Dorman TO69081XL (driver outer), TO69082XL (passenger outer) and TI69060XL (inner) [S4].
- **Brakes:** extended front brake lines (and rear lines if you lift the rear) [S1][S3][S4].
- **Cam bolts** for the lower arms [S3].
- **Bolts and nuts:** for an M205 swap, one owner's list from 2013 [S3]:
  - 12 half-shaft flange bolts (39606-17V0A) [S3];
  - 3 new 19 mm differential-to-frame bolts (54726-7S000) and 2 nuts (08918-3442A); the third bolt goes into a nut welded to the frame [S3];
  - 8 new 14 mm front driveshaft bolts (37120-4P00A) and nuts (37171-7S00A) [S3];
  - 2 new 32 mm hub nuts [S3].
  
  A 2021 list gives 39606-17V00 for the flange bolts, and 08918-6441A for the differential nuts [S2].
- **Gear oil:** one owner bought 3 quarts of 75W-90 synthetic for the M205 [S3]. Check the fill level with the differential level.
- **Small parts:** anti-seize for the cam bolts and suspension bolts, and new cotter pins for the half-shaft hub nuts; old ones get mangled coming out [S1].

## Front swap gotchas

- **The Titan steering boots don't fit.** The Titan rack is slightly larger: the Titan bellows' inner rings are 64 mm and the Xterra rack needs 61 mm. Keep Xterra boots (Nissan boot kit 48203-EA025) on the Titan tie rods [S2].
- **Tie rods may come without jam nuts.** One owner bought M16 grade 8 nuts at a hardware store, taking the inner tie rod along to check the fit [S2].
- **Center the steering wheel and align it promptly.** With the tie rods set up roughly, one owner's VDC read the off-center wheel as a turn: the slip light came on, it braked a wheel, and then VDC switched itself off. A careful home alignment fixed it [S1].
- **ABS sensor wires** may be too short with the extra travel. One owner, also running a 3 in body lift, spliced in about 12 in of wire on each side [S1].
- **Bump stops and limit straps:**
  - Owners recommend progressive bump stops. One owner drilled a new mounting hole about 1 in outboard of the factory one on the lower arm. Another mounted them on the bash plate, so they don't have to be moved again when the lower arms are changed [S2].
  - Limit straps are also recommended [S2].
- **Coilover wear:** after 45,000 miles, one owner's coilover lower spherical bearing was worn out and had mangled its bolt. A rebuild kit was $23 [S1].
- **Sway bar:** many swapped trucks run without one. One owner cut the sway bar mounts off his Titan lower arms because they hung below the arms [S2][S4].

## Full Titan front brakes and knuckles

One owner went further: Titan knuckles, hubs and brakes, for bigger brakes and the Titan 6 x 5.5 in lug pattern [S2].

- **Titan knuckles bolt straight to Titan-geometry arms.** No drop bracket is needed [S2].
- **Brake size sets the wheel size.** He used 2004-2006 Titan brakes to keep 17 in wheels. 2007-on Titans have larger brakes that need at least an 18 in wheel [S2].
- **Hubs:** his knuckles came from a 2012 Infiniti QX56, whose hubs have the larger studs used from 2010. Those didn't fit the 2006 Titan rotor, so he fitted 2006 Titan hubs. The Nissan hub 40202-7S100 turned up as a Timken SP500701 in a Nissan box, about $70 dearer [S2].
- **Master cylinder:** the Xterra master cylinder's bore is too small for the larger front calipers, so you need the Titan one, and he changed the booster to match.
  - On a manual, the clutch shares the master cylinder reservoir, and there was never a manual Titan. He combined a 2006 Titan master cylinder (from a truck with VDC) with an Xterra reservoir [S2].
  - The Titan master cylinder has two pressure sensors where the Xterra has one [S2].
- He reported the ABS pump has the same part number on both trucks [S2].
- **Wheels:** the Titan pattern means Titan or other 6 x 5.5 in wheels [S2][S7].

## Rear: a Titan M226 axle

The Titan rear axle is wider and has a different lug pattern and brakes. One owner who did it in 2021 [S2]:

- **Perches and mounts are in the wrong place.** The Titan's spring perches and shock mounts must be cut off and new ones welded on in the Xterra positions. He used RuffStuff R1328 perches, the only ones he found with the right inner diameter for the M226 [S2].
- **Do the gears first.** His donor, from a 2012 Titan Pro-4X, needed a pinion bearing and turned out to have a blown locker. Nisstec rebuilt it with a new locker and 4.56 gears [S2].
  - None of these axles came stock with 4.10 gears, so expect to regear any donor [S7]. See [Regearing](regearing.md).
- **ABS sensors:** Nissan changed the rear sensor connector in mid-2012, so he needed the 2011-and-earlier type; he used NTK AB0665 and AB0666 [S2].
- **Brake lines:** he had custom stainless lines made, including frame-to-axle lines 6 in longer than the stock Xterra ones [S2].
- **Parking brake:** use the Titan cables, but buy **two of the shorter cables**, because one side is much longer [S2].
  - Another owner found that shortening the handle-end cable failed every time: crimped, welded and soldered stops all let go. Only a factory cable stop held the tension, so don't cut it off.
  - He also welded the cable's spacer plate to the body, because the factory bolts flexed [S2].
- It's a lot of work. Owners point out that rear wheel spacers are much easier if all you want is matching track width [S7].

## Rear springs: weigh the truck first

- Owners agree on one thing: **weigh the loaded truck and order springs for the measured load** over stock. Packs ordered for too little load sagged quickly [S5].
- A stock PRO-4X 6-speed weighed 2,120 lb on the rear axle, with 4,460 lb gross [S5].
- One owner says Alcan doesn't like building over 4 in of lift for the second-generation Xterra; too much arch on a short spring is a bad combination [S5].
