# Timing chain and guide replacement (secondary chains and tensioner shoes)

[Guides](index.md) · [Reference home](../index.md) · Symptoms and background: [Timing chain whine](timing-chain-noise.md)

| | |
|---|---|
| Applies to | 2005-2015 Xterra, VQ40DE. Nissan's bulletin covers 2005-2010 [S1][S3] |
| Difficulty | Hard: the radiator, front accessories, crank pulley and front timing cover all come off. Nissan's bulletin is written for technicians [S1][S2] |
| Time | Nissan's labor time is 4.4 hours. One owner took a weekend, writing notes as he went [S1][S2] |

The bulletin replaces the two **secondary** chains and their tensioner shoes and **reuses the primary chain** [S1]. Nissan says neither the valve covers nor the intake plenum need to come off [S1][S3].

## Parts and supplies

- Secondary chains 13028-ZK01C (2), tensioner shoes 13097-ZK01C (2), front cover O-rings, crankshaft front seal 13510-7Y000, and sealant (Nissan lists ThreeBond 1217F). The full list is in [Timing chain whine](timing-chain-noise.md#parts-in-nissans-bulletin) [S1]
- About 7 qt of oil and a filter, and about 3 gallons of coolant (you won't use it all) [S2]
- Masking tape, a clean cotton towel, a white or permanent marker [S1][S2]
- Optional, since it's all off anyway: drive belt, belt tensioner, water pump, spark plugs [S2]

## Tools

- Metric sockets in 1/4, 3/8 and 1/2 in drive, wrenches, pry bars, a breaker bar with a cheater pipe or an impact wrench, a soft-faced hammer, drift pins, a torque wrench and angle gauge [S2]
- **Something to stop the engine turning.** Nissan uses ring gear stop J-48761 [S1]. Without it, one owner put a manual 4x4 in 6th and 4LO, or an automatic 4x4 in 4LO. On a 2WD automatic he wedged a large screwdriver in the flywheel through the access panel [S2].
- Pins to hold the three tensioner plungers compressed (in Nissan's J-50246 kit) [S1]
- A large hose clamp to press the new shoes onto the tensioner pistons, if you don't have J-50246 [S2]

## Procedure

**Teardown**

1. Start with a **cold** engine. Lock the drivetrain or fit a ring gear stop (see Tools). Disconnect the battery, negative first [S1][S2].
2. Remove the skid plates and front bumper, and drain the oil and coolant [S2].
3. Remove the radiator shroud's 3 top screws and the electric fan, then the fan shroud [S2].
4. With the belt still on, loosen the fan pulley nuts. Then remove the belt tensioner (one center bolt) and the belt [S2].
5. Remove the upper and lower radiator hoses and the coolant hoses to the engine, oil filter housing and throttle body [S2].
6. Unplug the throttle body, intake and MAF sensor. Remove the intake tube and tape over the opening [S2].
7. Remove the fan and tensioner bracket (4 bolts). Unbolt the alternator, power steering pump and A/C compressor and push them aside **without disconnecting their hoses** [S2].
   - One owner also removed the passenger front wheel and inner fender liner to get at the power steering pump and alternator [S2].
8. Set the engine to **0° TDC** on the crankshaft pulley, then remove the pulley bolt and pry the pulley off [S1][S2].
9. Remove the 25 front timing cover bolts and the 2 bolts in front of the oil pan [S2].
   - The passenger-side oil pan bolt may not clear on some trucks. One owner had to drop the front sway bar and loosen the steering rack to get it out [S2].
   - Watch for an idler pulley bolt that threads through into the cover [S2].
10. Pry the cover off carefully with several pry bars; the factory sealant is tough. Stuff a clean towel into the oil pan opening [S2].
11. **Check the timing marks.** If the camshaft and crankshaft sprocket mating marks don't line up after the cover is off, the crankshaft is 360° out. Refit the pulley and turn the crank one full turn [S1].
12. **Mark the primary chain** to all three sprockets with white-out or a paint marker. Pin all three tensioner plungers [S1][S2].

**Replace the secondary chains and shoes**

13. Nissan's bulletin removes and reinstalls the cam sprocket bolts **while all three chains are still on**, with the ring gear stop holding the crank [S1].
14. One side at a time [S2]:
    1. Remove the sprocket bolts and pry the sprockets off with the secondary chain [S2].
    2. Pry the old shoe off its tensioner **evenly**, leaving the plunger pinned [S1].
    3. Press the new shoe onto the tensioner with J-50246, tightening its bolt only until the shoe is fully seated. Without the tool, one owner pressed the shoe on with a large hose clamp and left the clamp on until the chain was fitted [S1][S2].
    4. On the **driver's side**, put the plunger in its bore before you fit the shoe, or it won't go back on [S2].
    5. Fit the new secondary chain to both sprockets with the keyways aligned, slide the assembly onto the camshafts until it seats, and start the bolts by hand [S2].
15. Dip new parts in fresh engine oil before fitting them [S2][S3].

**Reassembly**

16. Refit the primary chain to your marks, then the primary tensioner and the top guide [S2].
17. Torque the cam sprocket bolts with **all chains installed and the crank held** [S1][S3].
18. **Remove all three tensioner pins** before the front cover goes on [S1].
19. Clean every trace of old sealant off the block and cover without scratching the machined faces [S2].
20. Fit new O-rings in the block and on the cam access panels, re-seal the panels, and press in a new crankshaft seal [S1][S2].
21. Apply new sealant, refit the cover using drift pins in the large bolt holes to line it up, and torque the bolts and pulley as in the table [S2][S3].
22. Reassemble the rest. Refill with oil and a new filter and with coolant, then bleed the coolant (see [Coolant drain and refill](coolant-flush.md)) [S1][S2].
23. Reset the radio presets, power windows and clock [S1].

## After the job

- Make sure the sprockets are fully seated. One owner helped on a truck where a cam sprocket came loose afterwards [S2].
- Watch the **exhaust cam dowel pins** at reassembly; they can pop out [S2].
- One owner had a P0011 (intake valve timing) code 10,000 mi later that cleared after an oil change. It can come from dirty oil or a leak at the valve timing parts disturbed in the job [S2].
