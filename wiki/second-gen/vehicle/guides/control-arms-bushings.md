The ball joints are part of the arms. Nissan replaces the whole upper or lower link when a ball joint is worn [S1]; see [Ball joints and tie rods](ball-joints-tie-rods.md).

Nissan tightens bushing bolts with the truck at normal ride height, wheels on the ground and unladen. Torquing them with the suspension hanging pre-loads the rubber [S4].

Some trucks came with **straight, non-adjustable lower arm bolts**. Nissan says to replace them with cam bolts and washers before camber and caster can be set [S1]. Owners fit Nissan or PRG cam bolts [S3].

## Removing the upper arm

1. Lift the truck on stands, remove the wheel, and support the lower arm with a jack [S1].
2. **Left side only:** remove the pinch bolt from the steering shaft's lower joint at the steering gear and move the shaft aside without damaging it [S1].
3. Remove the cotter pin and nut from the upper ball joint, then separate the joint from the knuckle with a ball joint separator (Nissan tool ST29020001) [S1].
   - Owners use a 19 mm socket for this nut. They thread it back on a few turns, then strike the knuckle beside the joint to pop it loose [S5].
4. Remove the arm's mounting bolts and nuts [S1].
5. Refit with new nuts where the manual marks them, and torque them per the table [S1].

## Removing the lower arm

1. Remove the wheel and the lower shock bolt [S1].
2. Remove the end link's lower nut and separate it from the arm [S1].
3. On 4WD trucks, remove the drive shaft [S1].
4. Remove the pinch bolt from the knuckle and separate the lower ball joint from the knuckle [S1].
5. Remove the cam bolts and nuts, then the arm. Move the jounce bumper to the new arm [S1].

## When the lower arm bolts are seized

The bolt can rust into the steel sleeve of the arm's bushing. On a salted-road truck that can mean cutting the arm out [S3].

1. Soak the bolts with penetrating oil **several times over a few days** before you start. One owner soaked his daily for a week and still had one that wouldn't move [S3].
2. Relieve all load on the arm: remove the end link, jack under the arm, and clamp the arm toward the center of the truck. Disconnecting the upper ball joint takes the coil-over load off as well [S3].
3. Remove the 19 mm nut, thread it back on a few turns, and drive the bolt out with a 3-4 lb hammer on the nut. Or drive it out with another grade 8 bolt or an air hammer [S3].
4. If it won't move: a torch will free it but **destroys the bushing**, so plan on a new arm. Otherwise grind the arm beside the bushing and cut through the bolt **and** its sleeve with a reciprocating saw and plenty of blades. Protect the brake lines while you cut [S3].
5. On reassembly, coat the new bolts generously with **anti-seize** [S3].
6. Fit the cam bolts the same way round as the originals, nut facing forward. Work the arm until the eccentric washers drop into the raised guides on the mount [S3].
7. Fit the lower ball joint into the knuckle until its groove lines up with the pinch-bolt hole, or the bolt won't go through. Fit the pinch bolt from the front [S3].
8. Get an alignment [S1][S3].

One owner paid just under $200 for a Nissan lower arm in 2010; another paid about $125 each from a dealer's online parts store [S3].
