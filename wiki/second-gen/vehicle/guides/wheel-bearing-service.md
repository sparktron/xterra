# Wheel bearings: front hub assembly and rear axle bearings and seals

| Difficulty | Front: moderate, about 2 hours, plus extra time and a bigger hammer if it's rusted in [S4][S5]. Rear: hard, needs a press or a shop to press the bearing [S2][S6] |
| Interval | No maintenance interval. Nissan says the bearings need no maintenance: replace one when it growls or has end play [S1][S2] |

## Diagnosing a bad bearing

- **Front:** jack the wheel up, grab the hub and push and pull it along the axle. The end-play limit is **0.05 mm (0.002 in)**: there should be no play you can feel. Spin it and listen. If it's rough or noisy, replace the hub assembly [S1].
- **Rear:** shake each rear wheel for play. Nissan says to replace a rear bearing that **growls** while driving [S2].
- A rear axle seal that leaks puts gear oil on the brake. First pull and clean the axle **breather**: it sits on top of the axle on the passenger side and takes a 14 mm deep socket. A clogged breather lets the axle pressurize and push oil past the seals [S6].

## Front hub and bearing (2WD and 4WD)

**Tools:** 32 mm 6-point axle socket and a long breaker bar, 17 mm socket for the hub bolts (a deep or flex socket helps), 19 mm (3/4 in) socket for the caliper bracket, hammer or slide-hammer hub puller, wire brush, anti-seize [S4][S5].

The hub is the same part on both sides [S4].

1. Break the lug nuts loose and lift the truck on its frame jacking points. Remove the wheel [S5].
2. On 4WD trucks, break the 32 mm axle nut loose. An impact gun is easiest. Otherwise, have a helper hold the brakes, or put the wheel back on with its center cap off, lower the truck, and use a breaker bar through the wheel [S4][S5].
3. Remove the caliper bracket (torque member) bolts and hang the caliper with its bracket out of the way **without opening the brake lines**. Mark the rotor's position on the hub and pull the rotor [S1][S4][S5].
   - Don't press the brake pedal while the caliper is off [S1].
4. Remove the cotter pin and the axle nut. Use a new cotter pin at reassembly [S1].
5. Remove the ABS wheel sensor from the hub. Nissan says not to pull on its harness. Inspect its O-ring, clean the hole, and grease the O-ring at reassembly [S1].
   - The sensor's connector is notoriously hard to separate. One owner frees it by wedging a very small flat screwdriver into the slot about 1/2 in below the release tab [S5].
6. Free the axle from the hub's splines [S1][S4].
7. Remove the four 17 mm hub bolts from the back of the knuckle. The top two are awkward to reach [S1][S4][S5].
8. Drive or pull the hub out of the knuckle. Hit it **evenly** so it doesn't cock in the bore, or use a slide-hammer puller; parts stores rent them [S4][S5].
   - Rusty trucks fight hard. One owner needed a 5 lb sledge after 3 hours with a small hammer; soak it in penetrating oil overnight [S4].
9. Clean the knuckle bore with a wire brush or emery cloth [S5].
10. Refit the brake dust shield **before** the new hub if it came off with the old one [S5].
11. Put a little anti-seize on the axle splines and in the bore, then fit the new hub. It's an interference fit, so tap it in evenly while you line up the bolt holes [S4][S5].
12. Fit **new** hub bolts and torque them to 60 N·m (44 ft-lb) [S1][S4].
13. Refit the axle nut to 137 N·m (101 ft-lb), a new cotter pin, the rotor on its mark, the caliper and its anti-rattle clips, and the ABS sensor [S1][S4].

## Rear axle bearing and seal

Both rear axles hold the axle shaft in with nuts at the axle flange, and both press the bearing onto the shaft. **Nissan says to replace the bearing, its retainer ring and the axle seal every time a shaft comes out** [S2].

**Removing the shaft (both axles)** [S2]

1. Move the ABS wheel sensor out of the way first so the shaft doesn't damage it [S2].
2. Remove the wheel, hang the caliper without opening the lines, mark and remove the rotor, and remove the parking brake shoes [S2].
3. Remove the four nuts at the axle flange: the C200's axle bearing nuts, or the M226's bearing cage nuts and lock washers [S2].
4. Pull the shaft out with a slide hammer [S2][S6].
   - **M226 tip:** leave each nut just threaded on and tap its stud back out of the bearing cage with a soft punch. With the cage free, the shaft comes out much more easily and misses the ABS sensor. The studs have a straight knurl and press back in easily [S6].
   - On the M226 the bearing cup can stay in the housing. Remove it without scoring the housing [S2].

**Replacing the bearing (press work)**

- **C200:** press off the back plate and bearing, then press the new bearing and a new retainer ring on. Nissan's maximum press load is 98 kN (10 metric tons). Fit a new snap ring and a shim washer chosen for a snap ring to washer clearance of 0.2 mm (0.008 in) or less. Then do the break-in rotation and check the turning torque of 1.8 N·m (16 in-lb) [S2].
- **M226:** don't try to press the old retainer ring off. Drill a 1/4 in hole about 3/4 of the way through it (about 6.3 mm, 0.25 in deep) without reaching the shaft, then split it with a chisel. Nissan forbids heating or torch-cutting the bearing or ring during removal [S2][S6]. Press the new bearing on until a 0.038 mm (0.0015 in) feeler won't fit at its seat, then press the new retainer ring on with at least 31,100 N (6,992 lb) [S2].
  - One owner did the M226 without a press: a pipe of about 1-5/8 in ID and longer than 27-1/2 in, driven onto the bearing's **inner race only**. He used NAPA BR10 bearing and 45600 seal part numbers [S6].
  - That owner also heated the new retainer ring in an oven at 500°F for half an hour before fitting it [S6]. Nissan's manual presses it on cold [S2].
- **Check the seal surface on the shaft.** A groove worn by the old seal will leak again. Polish out a light ring with 180 then 400 grit, or fit a repair sleeve (SKF Speedi-Sleeve, Timken Redi-Sleeve) [S6].
- Fit the new seal facing the same way as the old one and grease its lip [S2][S6].

**Reinstalling:** grease the axle housing recess, oil the splines, guide the shaft through the new seal without damaging it, and torque the flange nuts evenly in a criss-cross pattern [S2]. Clean the brake parts thoroughly with brake cleaner if the old seal leaked oil onto them [S6].
