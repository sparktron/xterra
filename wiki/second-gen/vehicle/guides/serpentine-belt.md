## Parts

- Belt: Nissan 11720-EA200, superseded by 11720-EA20A; see [Replacement part numbers](../parts.md#serpentine-fan-and-alternator-belt) [S3]
- Tensioner and idler pulley, if they are noisy, rough or wobbly [S1]

## Checking the belt

1. With the **engine stopped**, remove the air duct and resonator assembly [S1].
2. Look at the auto-tensioner's indicator. It must sit inside the allowable range marked on the tensioner [S1].
3. Check the whole belt for wear, cracks and damage [S1].
4. Replace the belt if the indicator is out of range or the belt is damaged. There is **no manual tension adjustment**; the auto-tensioner sets it [S1].

## Replacing the belt

1. Remove the air duct and resonator assembly [S1].
2. **Photograph or sketch the routing** before you take anything off. Nissan's routing diagram is in the service manual on p. EM-13 [S1].
3. Put a breaker bar or serpentine-belt tool on the auto-tensioner and rotate it in its loosening direction [S1].
   - Keep your hands clear. If the tool slips, the tensioner snaps back [S1].
4. Slip the belt off a smooth pulley, release the tensioner slowly, and remove the belt [S1].
5. Route the new belt around every pulley, rotate the tensioner again, slip the belt over the last pulley and release it [S1].
6. Check that the belt sits squarely in every pulley groove [S1].
7. Refit the air duct and resonator [S1].

## Replacing the tensioner or idler

1. Remove the belt [S1].
2. Remove the auto-tensioner and idler pulley and fit the new ones [S1].
3. Torque the auto-tensioner bolts to **55.0 N·m (41 ft-lb)** and the idler pulley bolt to **34.8 N·m (26 ft-lb)** [S4].
4. Refit the belt. Nissan says to replace the auto-tensioner if it shows **any damage beyond peeled paint** [S1].

## Symptoms worth checking

- A chirp or squeal at start-up or under A/C load can be a glazed belt or a weak tensioner [Unverified].
- A rumble or whine that changes with engine speed, and goes away with the belt off, points to a pulley bearing. Spin each pulley by hand with the belt off and feel for roughness [Unverified].
- On these engines, a whine from the front of the engine is also a classic timing chain guide symptom. Rule out the belt first; see [Timing chain and guide wear](timing-chain-noise.md) [Unverified].
