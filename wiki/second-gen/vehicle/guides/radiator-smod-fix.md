## Bypass the radiator's transmission cooler

Read the trade-offs in [SMOD](smod.md#prevention-replace-the-radiator-or-bypass-its-cooler) first. Bypassing removes part of the transmission's cooling capacity [S5].

Work from under the truck, **lying on your back looking up at the radiator, with the driver's side on your left** [S2].

1. Remove the radiator skid plate or under cover. There is no need to remove the bumper [S1].
2. Put a pan under the radiator. Some ATF will drain from the hoses [S2].
3. **Hose A:** find the hose leaving the bottom **left (driver's side)** corner of the radiator, which runs back to the transmission line on the engine. Disconnect it **from the engine end** and let it hang [S2].
4. **Hose B:** find the hose at the bottom **right (passenger side)** of the radiator, which runs up into the front bumper to the separate air-to-oil cooler. Disconnect it **from the radiator** and push it onto the engine-side line you freed in step 3 [S2].
5. Connect the hanging end of hose A to the radiator nipple you freed in step 4. The radiator's internal cooler is now a closed loop with no flow through it, and the ATF runs only through the air-to-oil cooler [S2].
6. Check the hose clamps, top up the ATF, and check for leaks with the engine running [S2][S6].

**Optional second cooler:** one owner added an inexpensive auxiliary cooler in series behind the stock air cooler: ATF into the new cooler, out into the factory one, with no other hose changes [S1]. Another added a fan to the air cooler, because he found it does little below 20-25 mph [S5]. A [transmission temperature gauge](cooling-upgrades.md) lets you see whether the bypass is enough for your driving [S5].

After a bypass, owners often do a full ATF flush; see [ATF change and flush](atf-change.md#method-2-flush-through-the-cooler-line-nissans-method-owners-call-it-the-pmf) [S2].

## Replace the radiator

### Nissan's outline (2013 manual)

1. Drain the coolant from the radiator with the engine **cold**. Keep coolant off the drive belt [S7].
2. Remove the air duct, resonator and upper air cleaner case [S7].
3. Disconnect the reservoir hose, the upper and lower radiator hoses, and the A/T cooler hoses [S7].
4. Remove the electric (motor-driven) cooling fan and the front grille [S7].
5. Remove the upper radiator mounting bracket bolts and the two A/C condenser bolts [S7].
6. Lift the radiator and pull it rearward to free its lower mounting rubbers. Lift the A/C condenser **only as far as needed** to free its bottom fitting, then lift the radiator out [S7].
7. Rest the condenser on the core support and tie it up so the A/C lines aren't strained [S7].
8. Install in reverse. Warm the engine, check for coolant and ATF leaks, and correct both levels [S7].

| Item | Torque | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Radiator mounting bracket bolts, fan and shroud bolts | 4.2 N·m (37 in-lb) | [S7] |
| Radiator drain plug (new O-ring) | 1.2 N·m (11 in-lb) | [S7] |

### An owner's detailed sequence (2011, automatic)

This adds the details the manual skips [S4]:

1. **Check the old ATF first.** Drain some from the pan into a clear bottle and look for SMOD. Don't refill the transmission until the old radiator is out [S4].
2. Remove the bottom skid plate and drain the radiator at the drain cock [S4].
3. Disconnect both transmission cooler lines and plug them with bolts held in the existing clamps [S4].
4. Disconnect the battery negative. Remove the intake tube and air box, and remember the MAF connector. Cover the throttle body opening with a clean rag [S4].
5. From below, remove the **lower fan shroud**: push up on its two pins [S4].
6. Disconnect the lower, upper and reservoir hoses [S4].
7. Remove the **upper shroud and electric fan**. The belt-driven fan can stay on. Remove the shroud's 2 bolts and the fan's 1 bolt, unplug the fan, and free the cooler lines from the shroud clips. Push the shroud toward the engine to slip the fan out, then remove the shroud [S4].
8. Remove the 4 body clips holding the grille to the frame to reach the two 10 mm bolts of the upper radiator brackets. Remove both brackets with their rubber grommets [S4].
9. Unbolt the A/C condenser from the radiator (1 bolt per side). Stuff a rag below so a dropped bolt doesn't vanish. Tie the condenser to the front frame with rope [S4].
10. Lift and tilt the radiator back to free the condenser's bottom pins, and keep the condenser's 2 small bushings [S4].
11. From below, remove the 2 bolts holding the radiator to its long side support brackets. **Remove both brackets inside the engine bay**: the radiator won't clear the A/C piping with them attached. Then lift the radiator out [S4].
12. Transfer the square insert nuts at the bottom of the old radiator if the new one lacks them. Swap in new mounting rubbers and grommets now if you bought them [S4].
13. Refit the support brackets, then the radiator. Wiggle it until both lower mounts drop into their holes, and check that the condenser pins and bushings are seated [S4].
14. Zip-tie the lower support tabs to the truck so the brackets can't ride up. Fit the lower bolts loosely, **push the radiator up hard from below**, and tighten them while holding it up. The upper pegs are tapered, so this stops rattles at the top [S4].
15. Refit the condenser bolts, the upper brackets, the fan (bottom tabs in the radiator slots, passenger tab under the support bracket), the shrouds, and the hoses [S4].
16. Fill with coolant and bleed it; see [Coolant drain and refill](coolant-flush.md). Refill the transmission and check its level hot [S4].
