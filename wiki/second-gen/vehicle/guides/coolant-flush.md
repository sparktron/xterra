Nissan warns that mixing any other coolant into the blue Long Life coolant, including Nissan's own green Long Life, shortens its life. So does using water that is not distilled [S1]. Do not add sealers or other additives [S1].

## Tools and parts

- Drain pan that holds at least 3 gallons [Unverified]
- Enough coolant for the 10.2 L (about 2.7 US gal) system: pre-diluted Nissan blue, or concentrate plus distilled water [S1]. A radiator-only drain takes less [Unverified].
- Optional: a vacuum coolant refill tool. Nissan uses one (KV991J0070 / J-45695) running on 80-119 psi of dry compressed air [S1].
- Without the vacuum tool, a spill-free funnel kit that seals to the radiator neck. Owners use the Lisle Spill Free Funnel [S4].
- A small hose clamp or hose pinch-off pliers [S4]

## Draining

Work on a **cold** engine. Never open the radiator or reservoir cap hot: wrap a thick cloth around it, push down, and turn a quarter turn to release pressure first [S1].

1. Turn the ignition ON, set the heater to full HOT, wait 10 seconds, then turn the ignition OFF. This opens the heater circuit [S1].
2. Remove the engine under cover [S1].
3. Open the radiator drain plug at the bottom of the radiator and remove the reservoir cap. For a routine change, this radiator-only drain is all Nissan does [S1].
   - Keep coolant off the drive belt [S1].
4. For a full drain (engine work), Nissan also removes the cylinder block drain plugs. Refit them with thread sealant and new copper washers; never reuse the old washers [S1].
5. Remove the reservoir tank, empty and clean it, and refit it [S1].
6. Look at the drained coolant. If it shows rust, corrosion or discoloration, flush the system (below) [S1]. If it is **oily or pink**, ATF may be leaking into the coolant through the radiator's internal cooler; see [SMOD radiator failure](smod.md) [Unverified].

## Flushing with water (only if the old coolant was dirty)

1. Fill the radiator and the reservoir to MAX with water. Refit the radiator cap but leave the vented reservoir cap off [S2].
2. Run the engine to normal operating temperature, then blip the throttle two or three times with no load [S2].
3. Stop the engine, let it cool, and drain it again [S2].
4. Repeat until clear water drains from the radiator [S2].

## Refilling

**With a vacuum fill tool (Nissan's method)**

1. Close the radiator drain plug and refit anything you removed [S1].
2. Set the heater to full HOT and turn the ignition ON with the engine off [S1].
3. Swap the vented reservoir cap for a non-vented cap [S1].
4. Fit the tool to the radiator neck and pull vacuum to 28 inHg at sea level. Use less at altitude: 27 inHg at 984 ft, 26 at 1,641 ft, 24-25 at 3,281 ft [S1].
5. Disconnect the air hose and wait 20 seconds. If the vacuum drops, find the leak before you fill [S1].
6. Put the coolant container level with the top of the radiator and open the tool's valve. The system is full when the gauge reads zero [S1].
   - Don't let the container run low, or the tool will pull in air [S1].
7. Remove the tool and fit the radiator cap. Remove the non-vented reservoir cap, fill the reservoir to the specified level, run the engine warm, top up, and fit the vented cap [S1].

**Without the tool (funnel method, from a Nissan technician's procedure)**

1. Park nose-up on a slope, so the front of the truck is higher than the rear [S4].
2. Clamp the small hose that runs into the bottom of the reservoir. Otherwise coolant from the engine flows back into the reservoir through it [S4].
3. Release the pressure at the reservoir, remove the radiator cap, and fit the spill-free funnel to the radiator neck. Fill the radiator, then pour about 3 more cups of coolant into the funnel [S4].
4. Set the heater to full blast and run the engine up to operating temperature [S4].
5. Every 30 seconds or so, squeeze the large radiator hose and watch air bubbles come up into the funnel. Holding the engine at 2,000 rpm for 15-20 seconds also helps [S4].
6. When the bubbles stop, shut the engine off and let it cool. The coolant in the funnel drains back into the radiator [S4].
7. Remove the funnel, refit the radiator cap, make sure the reservoir cap is tight, and **remove the hose clamp** [S4].
8. Fill the reservoir between MIN and MAX with the engine cold [S2].

## After the job

- A **gurgling or "sucking" sound behind the dashboard** after a coolant job is air passing through the heater core. Repeat the funnel bleed until it is gone [S4].
- The system slowly bleeds itself. Keep checking the reservoir level cold for a few days and don't let it drop below MIN [S4].
- Read the reservoir level with the engine cold. A hot engine pushes the level up [S2][S4].
