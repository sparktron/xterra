# Power windows, door locks and keyfobs

[Guides](index.md) · [Reference home](../index.md) · Related: [Electrical faults](electrical-faults.md), [Fuses and relays](fuse-relay-map.md)

## Which fault is it?

| Symptom | Most likely | Where to look | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A rear (or passenger) window goes down from the driver's master switch but not back up, yet works from its own door switch | Dirty, worn contacts inside the master power window switch | [Repair the master switch](#repair-the-master-power-window-switch) | [S1][S2] |
| Driver's window randomly sticks down, then goes up if you hold the switch and close the door | A loose wire in the motor's plug in the door, or a binding or iced track | [Stuck window](#window-sticks-down-intermittently) | [S3] |
| Window slower and slower, then stops; struggles going up, needs a push | Window motor (a sealed unit) or regulator | [Replace the regulator or motor](#replacing-a-front-window-regulator-or-motor) | [S4][S5] |
| Driver's auto up/down misbehaves after glass, regulator or motor work | The motor's limit switch needs resetting | [Reset the limit switch](#reset-the-drivers-window-limit-switch) | [S6] |
| Keyfob stops locking and unlocking | Fob battery, or the fob's ID code lost from the body control module | [Keyfobs](#keyfobs) | [S7] |

## Repair the master power window switch

The driver's master switch is expensive new. In 2017 one owner found it cost more than $250 at dealers and about $175 from an online Nissan dealer, and another said cheap unbranded ones were about $70. Its contacts can usually be cleaned instead, in 30-45 minutes [S1][S2].

**You need:** electrical contact cleaner, fine sandpaper or a small wire brush, small plastic pry tools or two small flat screwdrivers, and a Phillips screwdriver [S1].

1. Pry up one end of the switch's trim panel on the door, lift it out and unplug it [S1].
2. Remove the 3 Phillips screws holding the switch to the trim panel [S1].
3. Pry the white cover off the switch, working one side loose and rotating it off. Don't jab the circuit board underneath [S1].
4. Pull off the **window lock** button to free the circuit board. **Leave the door lock button in place**: it has a small ball bearing on a spring that flies out if you remove it [S1][S2].
5. Lift out the green circuit board and spray it with contact cleaner, especially the gold contact stripes [S1].
6. For each window button you want to fix, pry off its small white cover. Under it are 2 metal "wing" rockers. Note which way they face (one end is larger), pull them out, clean the board contacts with a wire brush, and sand the rocker tips until they're bright. Doing one button at a time avoids mix-ups [S1].
7. The writer also bent the rocker ends down very slightly, on the theory that they bend up over time and lose contact [S2].
8. Spray once more, let everything dry completely, refit the rockers and covers, and reassemble [S2].

**If the door lock part falls apart:** one owner lost the ball bearing and a small white slider. The door lock switch's white arm pushes into that slider. Seat the slider by its notch and center it before refitting the switch. The bearing sits in grease on its spring [S2].

## Window sticks down intermittently

In one thread, the driver's window kept sticking down, and went back up when the owner held the switch and shut the door [S3].

- One owner who had exactly that replaced the regulator, motor and switches before finding the real fault: **a loose wire in the plug where the motor connects to the door harness**. Check the connections before buying parts [S3].
- To find it: with the door panel off, plug the switches back in, and wiggle the plug and its wires while you run the window [S3].
- Another owner suggested a binding or iced track. The original poster's episodes were all on frosty mornings, so he sprayed silicone on the track and pulleys [S3].
- Grease the track and put dielectric grease in the connector while you're in there [S3].

## Replacing a front window regulator or motor

The driver's window regulator is a cable type with the motor bolted to it. Nissan's 2009 manual lists **wire wear, a deformed regulator and old grease** as the things to inspect [S6].

**Parts and cost:** one owner bought a $40 replacement from RockAuto in 2018. Another paid $30.05 for an aftermarket regulator and $60.50 for a motor in 2013. A third used a Dorman 741-348 regulator-and-motor assembly ($55 in 2013) so he didn't have to swap the motor over; check its fitment for your door [S4][S5].

**Test the old motor first.** One owner swapped the regulator, then found the motor was the problem: it ran on a 12 V bench supply but no longer had the torque to lift the glass. Plug a new motor into the switch before you take the window out, to prove both the diagnosis and the part [S5].

**Tools:** 10 mm socket, door panel tool, razor knife, silicone sealant, grease, and masking tape. About 45 minutes [S4].

### Door panel off

1. Pop out the window switch panel and the small pad behind the pull handle, and unplug the switch [S4][S5].
2. Remove the two 10 mm screws behind them [S4].
3. Pry off the two plastic trim pieces around the inside door handle and remove the bolt behind them. One owner found Phillips and 10 mm bolts on the handle, and another said only the two 10 mm screws at its end need to come out [S4][S5].
4. Pop the plastic clips around the bottom and sides of the panel with a long screwdriver or panel tool, then lift the panel up and off [S4][S5].
5. Remove the 4 10 mm screws holding the two metal panel brackets [S4].
6. Cut or peel the foam vapor barrier off. It's stuck down with a bead of sealant; one owner cut it free with a razor knife, which took 30 minutes, and another found it peels more easily when the door is warm [S4][S5].

### Regulator out and in

1. Plug the switch back in and run the window down until the **2 glass bolts** show through the access holes, about three-quarters of the way down [S4][S5][S6].
2. Remove the glass bolts, push the glass up to fully closed, and tape it there [S4][S5].
   - If the window is stuck up, reaching the glass bolts is hard [S5].
3. Unplug the motor (press the latch down with a small screwdriver) [S5].
4. Remove the 7 10 mm bolts: 4 for the regulator's track (2 top, 2 bottom) and 3 around the motor [S4][S5].
5. Lower the assembly out of the large opening at the bottom of the door [S4].
6. If you're reusing the motor, it comes off the regulator with 3 Phillips screws [S5].
7. Grease the new regulator's tracks [S4].
8. Fit the new assembly. Line the track bolts up with the wear marks of the old ones so the glass stays aligned [S4]. Nissan's order is:
   1. the top rear regulator bolt, loosely; [S6]
   2. the top front, bottom front and bottom rear; [S6]
   3. tighten the top rear; [S6]
   4. the top, lower and rear motor bolts. [S6]
   
   **Regulator and motor bolts: 5.7 N·m (50 in-lb)** [S6].
9. Plug in the motor. Lower the glass onto the regulator, making sure it's in both the front and rear glass channels. Tighten the **glass bolts to 6.1 N·m (54 in-lb)** [S6].
10. Run the window up and down a few times. Nissan's check: lower the glass about 10-20 mm (0.39-0.79 in) and see that the gap to the door sash is even. If not, loosen the regulator and guide rail bolts and reposition the glass [S4][S6].
11. On the driver's door, [reset the limit switch](#reset-the-drivers-window-limit-switch) [S6].
12. Re-seal the foam barrier with a fresh bead of silicone along the old line and around the wires, then refit the panel brackets and the panel [S4].

## Reset the driver's window limit switch

The driver's window motor has a built-in limit switch. Nissan says to reset it after removing or refitting the regulator, the motor, the glass or the glass run [S6].

1. Raise the glass to the top [S6].
2. Hold in the **reset switch on the motor** and lower the glass to the bottom [S6].
3. Release the reset switch and check that it springs back; pull it out with a suitable tool if not [S6].
4. Raise the glass to the top. **Don't use auto-up for this** [S6].

## Door lock assembly

Nissan's 2013 procedure for the front door lock [S7]:
1. Remove the door panel and the rear glass run channel [S7].
2. Remove the bolt for the key cylinder (driver) or handle escutcheon (passenger) through the grommet hole in the side of the door [S7].
3. Pull the outside handle while you remove the key cylinder or escutcheon, then slide the handle rearward and out [S7].
4. Remove the lock's bolts and slide the lock and outside handle bracket out together [S7].
5. Unplug the actuator [S7].

- **Door lock bolts: 5.8 N·m (51 in-lb). Striker bolts: 16.7 N·m (12 ft-lb).** Nissan marks both as **do not reuse** [S7].
- When refitting, turn the key cylinder rod holder until it clicks, and don't twist the door lock cable [S7].
- The body control module drives all the door lock actuators [S7].

## Keyfobs

**Battery check:** open the fob with a coin, without touching the circuit board. Nissan tests the battery under a load of about 300 Ω (about 10 mA): **2.5-3.0 V is good**. Fit the new battery positive side down, and keep the contacts clean [S7].

**Program a fob without a scan tool** (Nissan's procedure). The truck stores up to 5 fob codes, and a new one pushes out the oldest [S7].

1. Close all doors [S7].
2. Insert the key fully in the ignition and pull it fully out, **more than 6 times within 10 seconds**. The hazards flash twice. Going too fast won't enter programming mode [S7].
3. Insert the key and turn it to ACC [S7].
4. Press any button on the fob once. The hazards flash twice and the fob is registered [S7].
5. For each extra fob: press unlock, then lock, on the driver's master switch, then press any button on the next fob (hazards flash twice) [S7].
6. Open the driver's door to finish, then check every fob works [S7].

**Lost fob:** register one fob 5 times to wipe every stored code, then register every fob you still have [S7].
