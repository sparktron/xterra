## Thermostat

Nissan sells the thermostat and its housing ("water inlet") as **one assembly**. Don't take it apart [S1]. In 2008 one owner found only the dealer had it [S2].

**Tools:** a 10 mm socket on a short extension, pliers for the hose clamps, a flat screwdriver, and about 1 gallon of coolant [S2].

1. With the engine **cold** and the system not under pressure, put a pan underneath [S2].
2. Remove the engine cover (two 10 mm bolts at the front; the rest snaps off). Remove the intake tube: unplug the MAF, loosen the clamp at the throttle body, unhook the air box lid, and disconnect the vacuum line [S2].
3. Nissan also removes the electric cooling fan for access [S1].
4. Pull the lower radiator hose and the oil cooler hose off the thermostat housing [S1][S2].
5. Remove the 3 × 10 mm bolts and the housing. Coolant will pour out [S2].
6. Scrape off the old gasket, which is brittle and tends to stay stuck to the engine [S2]. Nissan specifies a **new gasket**; one owner reused his [S1][S2].
7. Fit the new assembly. A bolt pushed through as a guide helps line up the gasket. Torque the bolts to 8.4 N·m (74 in-lb) [S1][S2].
8. Refit the hoses and intake, refill the coolant, and bleed it; see [Coolant drain and refill](coolant-flush.md) [S2].

**Testing an old thermostat:** at room temperature the valve should seat tightly. Nissan's bench test hangs it on a thread in a pot of water and heats the water, checking where it opens and closes against the table above [S1][S3].

## Water pump (Nissan's procedure, condensed)

Nissan says the pump can't be rebuilt; replace it as a unit [S1].

1. Drain the coolant with the engine cold. Keep coolant off the timing chain and drive belt [S1].
2. Remove the air duct and resonator, the drive belt, the upper radiator hose, the reservoir hose, and the electric fan [S1].
3. Set **No. 1 cylinder at TDC**: turn the crank clockwise to line up the uncolored groove on the pulley with the timing indicator [S1].
4. Remove the belt-driven cooling fan [S1].
5. Remove the front water drain plug on the pump side of the block [S1].
6. Cut the sealant and remove the chain tensioner cover and the water pump cover from the front timing chain case [S1].
7. **Pin the primary chain tensioner:** release its plunger stopper, push the plunger in by pressing the slack guide, slide a stopper pin through the body and plunger groove, then unbolt the tensioner. Don't drop the bolts into the timing case [S1].
8. Turn the crank **counterclockwise** a little until the chain goes slack on the pump sprocket. Remove the 3 pump bolts [S1].
9. Thread two M8 × 1.25 bolts, about 50 mm (1.97 in) long, into the pump's upper and lower holes until they push against the timing case, jacking the pump out. Lay a rag below to keep coolant out of the timing case, and pull the pump straight out without the sprocket touching the chain [S1].
10. Fit **new O-rings**, oiled, with the white-paint-marked O-ring toward the front. Hold the chain aside, fit the pump with the sprocket engaged in the chain, and tighten the bolts alternately [S1].
11. Turn the crank about 20° clockwise so the chain is slack on the tensioner side, fit the tensioner with its pin in, then **pull the pin**. Check that the chain is still on the pump sprocket [S1].
12. Clean the cover faces, run a continuous bead of RTV, and fit the covers **within 5 minutes**. Don't add engine oil for at least 30 minutes while the sealant cures [S1].
13. Refit the drain plug with RTV on its threads, then everything else. Refill and bleed the coolant [S1].
14. Idle for 3 minutes, then rev to 3,000 rpm with no load to purge air from the tensioner. A rattle while it purges is normal [S1].
