## Clutch pedal check and adjustment (Nissan's procedure)

1. Check that the **clevis pin floats freely** in the pedal bore. If it binds, the cruise-control (ASCD) clutch switch is probably pushing on the pedal: loosen its lock nut and back the switch off [S1].
2. With the clevis pin out, the pedal should swing freely. If it sticks, replace the pedal assembly [S1].
3. With the pedal **fully down**, set the clearance between the pedal stopper rubber and the threaded end of the clutch interlock switch to **0.1-1.0 mm (0.004-0.039 in)** [S1].
4. Check the master cylinder, slave cylinder, release fork and release bearing for binding [S1].

**Shorter clutch throw:** owners make an adjustment behind the clutch pedal to change its throw, a common change on related Nissans such as the 370Z and G35. Getting behind the pedal is awkward. **Keep some free travel at the top of the pedal stroke** [S4]. The original instructions are on ClubFrontier [S4].

## Bleeding the clutch (Nissan's procedure)

1. Fill the reservoir with fresh brake fluid, and keep it from running empty [S1].
2. Fit a clear tube from the slave cylinder's bleeder into a container. The bleeder takes an 8 mm wrench [S1][S3].
3. Pump the pedal several times, hold it down, open the bleeder, then **close it before the pedal comes up** [S1][S3].
4. Repeat until clear fluid with no bubbles comes out, then torque the bleeder to 7.9 N·m (70 in-lb) [S1].

When the lines are full of fluid again, pressure builds behind the bleeder. Hold the tube on firmly so it doesn't blow off [S6].

## Rusty clutch lines: an owner's lessons

- When scraping rust underneath, **don't knock the rusty hydraulic lines**. Keep a drain pan and absorbent nearby [S6].
- Use **flare-nut wrenches**; ordinary wrenches round the fittings [S6].
- Cut out only the bad section and splice it with a threaded union, rather than pulling the whole line. The plastic body clips break [S6].
- If you make lines: buy a quality flaring tool and a tube bender, practice on scrap, and **flare before you bend**. Make sure each tube seats straight in its fitting. A new flare may need tightening and loosening 2-3 times before it seals [S6].
- Have the parts counter read part numbers back to you [S6].

## Neutral position sensor (6-speed)

Symptoms: **cruise control won't stay set** because the sensor falsely reports neutral, or the transfer case **won't go into 4LO** because it falsely reports in gear [S5].

- Part: Nissan 32006-CD10B (about $21 in 2017) [S5].
- It's on the **passenger side of the transmission, toward the engine**. Unplug it from below, slide its white plastic strain-relief off the hex by hand, and unscrew it with a 19 mm (3/4 in) wrench [S5].
- One owner removed the shifter knob, cup holder, trim and the 4 boot-ring bolts for access from above; two bolts were seized. He drilled them, tapped them M6-1.0, and fitted stainless M6-1.0 × 12 mm bolts [S5].

## Removing the transmission for a clutch job (Nissan's outline)

1. Disconnect the battery. Remove the shifter, the left fender liner, and the **crankshaft position sensor** from the transmission without damaging its tip [S2].
2. Remove the under cover, front crossmember, starter, both driveshafts, both front exhaust pipes, and the slave cylinder [S2].
3. Support the transmission on a jack, remove the crossmember, then tilt the transmission to unplug the breather hoses and the switches: backup lamp, PNP, ATP, neutral-4LO, wait detection and transfer control [S2].
4. Remove the transmission-to-engine bolts and pull the transmission back off the dowels. Then separate the transfer case [S2].
5. Clutch: remove the cover bolts and the disc. Don't clean the disc with solvent, and keep grease off the facing. Grease the input splines lightly with lithium grease with molybdenum disulfide, center the disc with an alignment tool, and torque the cover bolts in two steps in sequence [S1].
6. Check the flywheel for burns and runout. If the flywheel comes off, line the dowel pin up with its smallest hole [S1].
7. Refit the transmission at 75 N·m (55 ft-lb) with the dowels in place, so that the input shaft doesn't hit the clutch cover. Refill and check the transmission oil [S2].
