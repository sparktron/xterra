## Specifications

| Item | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Fluid | Genuine Nissan Super Heavy Duty Brake Fluid or equivalent, DOT 3 (FMVSS No. 116) | [S2][S3] |
| Bleed valve torque | 7.85 N·m (69 in-lb) | [S1] |
| Bleeding order | right rear, left front, left rear, right front | [S1] |

On manual-transmission trucks the clutch hydraulics are fed from the same reservoir, so flush the clutch line too (below) [S4]. A full flush of brakes and clutch used about 24 oz of a 32 oz bottle on one owner's 2011 [S4].

## Tools

- About 24 in of 3/8 in ID clear tube for the bleed valves, and a jar to catch fluid [S1][S4]
- 10 mm wrench or socket for the brake bleed valves; 8 mm for the clutch bleeder on a manual [S4]
- Something to suck old fluid out of the reservoir's small opening, such as a turkey baster with a short piece of tube sealed into its tip [S4]
- Penetrating oil. Bleed valves can seize; one owner found two very tight at 30,000 mi and freed them with Kroil and light taps on the ratchet [S4]
- A helper on the pedal [S1]
- Fresh fluid from a sealed bottle. **Never reuse fluid that has drained out** [S1]
- Water nearby. Brake fluid damages paint, so wash any splash off at once [S1]

## Before you start

Turn the ignition OFF and disconnect either the ABS actuator and electric unit connector or the battery negative terminal. Nissan does this before every brake-fluid job [S1]. One owner unplugged only the ABS connector. He had read in the manual's General Information section that reconnecting the battery can require a steering angle sensor reset with Nissan's CONSULT tool [S4].

Pulling the wheels makes the bleed valves much easier to reach [S4].

## Draining and refilling (full fluid change)

1. Fit a vinyl tube to each bleed valve [S1].
2. Press the pedal, open each bleed valve, and let the old fluid out gradually [S1].
3. Suck most of the old fluid out of the reservoir, check it for debris, then fill it with new fluid [S1][S4]. On a manual, a baffle in the reservoir keeps fluid over the clutch feed, so you won't draw air into the clutch line [S4].
4. With a foot resting on the pedal, open a bleed valve and press the pedal slowly to the floor. Close the valve, then release the pedal. Repeat a few times, topping up the reservoir between rounds, until new fluid comes out [S1].
5. Then bleed the air out as below [S1].

## Bleeding (Nissan's two-person method)

Keep the reservoir **at least half full** the whole time. Running it dry pulls air into the system [S1].

1. Fit the vinyl tube to the **right rear** bleed valve [S1].
2. The helper pumps the pedal fully 4-5 times, then holds it down [S1].
3. With the pedal held down, open the bleed valve to let the air out, then **close it immediately** [S1].
4. Repeat steps 2-3 until no more air comes out [S1].
5. Torque the bleed valve to 7.85 N·m (69 in-lb) [S1].
6. Repeat at the **left front**, then the **left rear**, then the **right front** [S1].
7. Top up the reservoir to between MIN and MAX. Reconnect the ABS connector or the battery [S1].
8. Before driving, check that the pedal is firm and that the brake warning lamp goes out with the parking brake released [S1].

## Clutch line (manual transmission)

1. Fit the clear tube to the clutch bleeder on the transmission (8 mm) [S4].
2. The helper pumps the clutch pedal a few times and holds it down [S4].
3. Open the bleeder, let the pedal go down, then close the bleeder **before** the helper lets the pedal up [S4].
4. Repeat until clean, bubble-free fluid comes out, keeping the reservoir topped up [S4].

## If the pedal is still soft

- Bleed again in the same order. Air often hides in the rear circuit [Unverified].
- If the reservoir level keeps dropping, look for a leak. If the brake warning lamp stays on with the parking brake released, Nissan says to check the system for leaks [S1].
- An ABS module that has had air pulled into it may need the dealer's scan-tool bleed routine [Unverified].
