## Torque values

| Fastener | 2012-2013 manuals | 2006 manual | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Front bar mounting bracket bolts | 150 N·m (111 ft-lb) | 130 N·m (96 ft-lb) | [S1][S2] |
| Front end link (connecting rod) nuts, both ends | 91 N·m (67 ft-lb) | 83.5 N·m (62 ft-lb) | [S1][S2] |
| Rear bar clamp bolts | 49 N·m (36 ft-lb) | 49 N·m (36 ft-lb) | [S3][S6] |
| Rear end link nuts, replaced and greased | 44 N·m (32 ft-lb) | 44 N·m (32 ft-lb) | [S3][S6] |

Torque the bushing fasteners with the truck at ride height on its wheels [S3].

## Front bar and bushings (Nissan's procedure)

1. Remove the engine under cover [S1].
2. Remove the end link nuts [S1]. One owner used a 17 mm socket on the link and a second wrench to hold the nut on the inside of the lower arm mount [S7].
3. Loosen the **top** bolts of the bar's mounting brackets, remove the **lower** bolts, and lower the bar [S1].
4. Remove the bushings. Check the bar for twist and the rubber for cracks and wear [S1].
5. Reassemble in reverse and torque to the table [S1].

An owner's transcription of the 2005 manual says to replace the front end link nuts after removal. He doubted it was needed [S8]. The 2013 manual marks the **rear** end link nuts as replace-after-removal [S3].

## Rear bar: removing or reinstating it

- Many owners pull the rear bar to free up articulation off road [S5].
- One owner argues for keeping it. With no rear bar, the rear cycles so freely that the front stops working: the truck leans with the rear axle and is more likely to slide off-camber [S5].
- **On a lifted truck,** the stock rear links are too short. Bolt the bar on, set its ends 10-20° below horizontal, and measure the end link's center-to-center length at that angle. Buy adjustable links whose range spans that length (Whiteline and SuperPro, 10 mm bolts), set both links equally, and tighten the jam nuts. He torques them to about 30-34 ft-lb [S5].
- That owner gave up 1-2 in of rear up-travel and gained full front articulation, about 10 in [S5].

## Front bar and articulation

- One owner measured front wheel travel with and without the front bar on a lifted truck. He found 4.5 in with the bar and 7 in without it, all of the gain in up-travel. He reinstalled the bar for road safety [S4].
- There's no room to swing a disconnected front bar out of the way, so quick-disconnect end links are less practical than on a solid-axle truck. One owner has been developing a center disconnect [S9].
- On a lifted truck the stock front links can bind or bottom the bar at full droop, which shows up as a **clunk**. Extended links fix it [S10].
