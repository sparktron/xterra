## Front coil-over removal and installation

**Tools:** 19 mm socket for the upper ball joint nut, 9/16 in socket or wrench for the three upper mount nuts, 3/4 in socket for the lower shock bolt, a jack for the lower arm [S5][S6].

1. Chock the rear wheels, break the lug nuts loose, lift the front onto stands under the frame, and remove the wheels [S6].
2. Remove the sway bar end links' **lower** bolts and swing the bar forward out of the way [S1][S6].
3. Free the ABS sensor wire from its three clips so it isn't stretched [S5][S6].
4. Support the lower arm with a jack [S1].
5. The manual removes the coil-over without disconnecting the upper ball joint; turn the knuckle out for clearance [S1]. Owners often disconnect the upper ball joint as well, for more room: remove its cotter pin and 19 mm nut, thread the nut back on, tap the knuckle to pop the joint, then remove the nut and let the upper arm swing up [S4][S6].
6. Remove the three upper mount nuts on top of the coil bucket, rear ones first [S1][S6].
   - **Do not remove the center nut** on the shock shaft. It holds the compressed spring [S5][S6].
7. Remove the lower shock bolt and nut, then lift the coil-over out [S1][S6].
8. Installation is the reverse [S1]:
   - The step in the lower spring seat faces the outside of the truck. A marking on the upper mount shows which way faces the engine [S1][S6].
   - Fit the top nuts first, rear ones first and front last, then jack the lower arm up to line up the lower bolt [S6].
   - One owner jacks the lower arm to seat the shock eye against the bolt, torques the top nuts and then the lower nut, and uses blue thread locker [S4].
   - Torque everything to the table below and fit a new cotter pin to the ball joint [S1].
9. Recheck all fasteners after the first 10 miles [S6].

## Rebuilding a front coil-over (spring or shock replacement)

**Compressing a coil spring is dangerous.** If you don't have spring compressors and know how to use them, have a shop do it. One owner's local shop swapped both springs for $40 in about 45 minutes [S4][S6].

Notes from an owner who built OME coil-overs [S4]:

- Replace at least the two rubber parts, the spring insulator seat and the upper bushing, unless the truck is new.
- Stack every part: lower washer, tube spacer, spring insulator, spring bracket, upper bushing, upper washer and lock nut. **Both washers go concave side down.** A missing or flipped washer lets the spacer move and the bushings compress unevenly. The coil-over still looks right while the spring holds it together.
- **Align the bracket before releasing the spring.** The stud at the apex of the triangle points toward the step in the spring seat, square to the lower eye. Otherwise the coil-over is hard to fit or goes in pre-loaded.
- On Off-Road and PRO-4X trucks, don't reuse the factory shock cover and adapter with an aftermarket shock. The adapter lets the lower arm droop further, and the upper arm can hit the coil bucket. [S4]

## Rear shocks

1. Support the axle and remove the upper and lower nuts and bolts [S3].
2. Grease the upper nut and stud, fit the new shock, and torque the nuts with the truck at ride height [S3].

Nissan's disposal note for gas shocks: drill a 2-3 mm (0.08-0.12 in) hole 20-30 mm (0.79-1.18 in) from the top of the body with the rod extended, to release the gas. The gas is harmless; wear eye protection [S1][S3].

## Bump stops

- **Energy Suspension** replacement bump stops (9.9101G and 9.9102G) fit on the lower and upper arms. Drill out the mounting hole, and on the upper arm hold a 3/8-16 wing nut behind it and thread the stop in with red thread locker [S8].
- **Timbren** rear bump stops are popular for towing, and to help the front under a winch bumper [S5].
- Lifted trucks often need extended bump stops to keep the shocks or CV axles from being the stop [Unverified].
