## Camshaft position sensors

1. Prop the hood higher than its stay allows; one owner used a 3 ft length of 2×4. Lay a blanket over the engine and use a step stool [S1].
2. Remove the engine cover (two 10 mm bolts) [S1].
3. The **driver's-side (bank 2, P0345)** sensor is at the back of the head with 3-4 in of clearance to the firewall. It isn't reachable from below past the catalytic converter and transmission [S1]. The passenger-side sensor is much harder to reach [S1].
4. Unplug the connector. It can be stubborn: push it in firmly until the latch releases, then pull. It's easier to unplug with the sensor still bolted in [S1].
5. Remove the 10 mm bolt and its washer, and pull the sensor [S1].
6. Fit the new sensor and torque the bolt to about 86 in-lb, snug only [S1].

If a new sensor doesn't stop the stalling, keep looking. One owner's stalling continued after both cam sensors were replaced [S1]. Another's turned out to be the crank sensor [S2]. On 2005-2006 trucks, Nissan's IPDM relay recall is worth checking first (free at a dealer); see [Recalls](../recalls.md) [S2].

## Crankshaft position sensor

**Tools:** 10 mm socket with a short extension, Phillips and flat screwdrivers, jack and stands [S2].

**Buy the right part:** parts sites list two sensors as "crankshaft position sensor". The real crank sensor was the cheaper of the two (about $20 in 2015); the other is the cam sensor [S2].

1. Let the engine and catalytic converter cool [S2].
2. Lift the front passenger corner and remove the wheel, then the screws holding the inner fender liner, about 10 [S2]. Some owners just pop the liner back or reach down from the top with an extension [S2].
3. Peel back the rubber boot over the sensor [S2].
4. Unplug the connector with a flat screwdriver. One owner found the new sensor's connector clip faced the opposite way [S2].
5. Remove the 10 mm bolt (if you drop it, it falls to the ground) and pull the sensor [S2].
6. Fit the new sensor and bolt, reconnect it, refit the boot, the liner and the wheel [S2].

## Oil pressure sensor (gauge sender)

2005-2006 trucks have a real pressure sender that drives the gauge; later trucks use an on/off pressure switch [S3]. One owner's 2005 part was Nissan **25070-CD00A** [S3].

1. Find the sender on the passenger side of the engine, below the alternator and behind the steering rack. It's visible from underneath [S3].
2. Unplug it by hand [S3].
3. Unscrew it with a 24 mm (15/16 in) deep socket or open-end wrench. Expect a small gush of oil, under 1/4 cup [S3].
4. Coat the threads with **paste pipe-thread sealant, not PTFE tape**: the sender grounds through its threads, and tape shreds can get into the oil passages [S3].
5. Fit the new sender. It has a tapered pipe thread, so don't over-tighten [S3]. For reference, the 2013 manual's oil pressure **switch** is torqued to 14.7 N·m (11 ft-lb) [S5]; the 2005-2006 sender may differ [Unverified].
6. Plug it in, start the engine, check the gauge and look for leaks [S3].

## Air/fuel ratio sensors

- Torque: **50.0 N·m (37 ft-lb)**. Over-tightening can damage the sensor and set the check engine light [S4].
- Before fitting a new sensor, clean the threads with an oxygen-sensor thread chaser and use anti-seize [S4].
- **Throw away any A/F sensor dropped more than 0.5 m (19.7 in) onto a hard floor** [S4].

## Knock sensors

The knock sensors sit under the intake manifold. Their bolts are **18.1 N·m (13 ft-lb)** [S4].
