The alternator uses a **variable charging voltage**, so the dash or a meter can show anywhere from about 11.4 to 15.6 V in normal running [S1]. Nissan says to run its power generation variable voltage control inspection after replacing the alternator [S1].

## Replacing the alternator

**Access tip:** remove the **passenger front wheel and the inner fender liner** (about 6 screws and 4 plastic retainers). The alternator sits right behind the liner [S4][S6].

1. Disconnect the battery negative terminal. Note your radio presets [S1][S4].
2. Remove the engine cover (2 bolts) and the intake tube (clamps at both ends, 2 bolts) [S4].
3. Release the belt tensioner with a 3/8 in drive ratchet or breaker bar and slip the belt off the alternator [S4]. Nissan's manual also removes the fan shroud [S1].
4. Lift the passenger front corner onto a stand and remove the wheel and inner fender liner [S4].
5. Remove the **short lower bolt** through the stay bracket, loosen the bracket's other bolt so it swings down, and **loosen** the long upper bolt [S1][S4].
6. Behind the coil bucket, unplug the alternator's connector and remove its ground / B-terminal nut. Free the wiring bundle clipped to the back of the alternator: undo the clips' zip ties rather than prying the clips off [S1][S4].
7. Remove the long upper bolt and work the alternator out [S1][S4].
8. Move the wiring clips to the new unit, fit it, and torque the bolts and terminal nut to the table [S1][S4].
9. Refit the belt, check its tensioner indicator, refit the intake and engine cover, reconnect the battery, and start it up. Then refit the fender liner and wheel [S1][S4].

## Rebuilding the alternator bearings

An owner's rebuild of a 2005 Off-Road alternator [S5]:

**Tools:** impact wrench, strap wrench, 24, 18, 10 and 8 mm sockets, a small gear puller, a flat screwdriver, a dead-blow hammer, a piece of stiff wire, a wood block, 600-grit wet/dry paper and penetrating oil [S5].

**Bearings:** one owner's 2005 Off-Road unit had a 6303 front bearing (17 × 47 × 14 mm; SKF 6303-2RSJ) and a 10 × 27 × 11 mm rear bearing (NSK B10-50D) [S7].

1. Soak the pulley nut with penetrating oil for a few hours. Hold the pulley with a strap wrench and remove the nut with a 24 mm socket on an impact wrench. Pull the pulley if needed [S5].
2. Remove the 4 long through-bolts and tap the front housing off evenly. Lift the rotor out; the rear bearing comes with it [S5].
3. Remove the 5 nuts on the back (8 mm), the plastic cover by the positive terminal and the 10 mm nut under it, then tap off the rear housing [S5].
4. Remove the 3 screws and the bearing plate from the front housing, and drive the front bearing out with an 18 mm socket [S5].
5. Pull the rear bearing off the rotor shaft with a small puller, **without damaging the slip rings** [S5].
6. Polish the slip rings with a strip of 600-grit paper wrapped around both, turning the rotor one way. Clean the parts with a non-corrosive cleaner, rinse and dry [S5].
7. Tap the new bearings into the end and front housings and refit the bearing plate [S5].
8. Check that the brushes slide freely. Push them in and hold them with a wire through the hole, fit the rotor, then pull the wire [S5].
9. Slide the end housing on without pushing the rotor forward, fit the shaft spacer, line up the front housing's mounting tabs and bolt holes, and fit the through-bolts [S5].
10. Refit the pulley and torque its nut to 118 N·m (87 ft-lb) [S1].

## Replacing the starter (Nissan's procedure)

1. Disconnect the battery negative terminal [S2].
2. Remove the starter cover bolts and cover [S2].
3. Unplug the "S" terminal connector and remove the "B" terminal nut [S2].
4. Remove the two starter bolts and the starter [S2].
5. Install in reverse. Torque the bolts to 45 N·m (33 ft-lb) and tighten the B-terminal nut carefully [S2].
