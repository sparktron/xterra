|---|---|---|---|
| 2WD, P265/70R16 | 839 mm (33.03 in) | 872 mm (34.33 in) | [S1] |

| 4WD, P265/75R16 | 870 mm (34.25 in) | 904 mm (35.59 in) | [S1] |

## The common ways to lift

| Approach | What it is | Typical lift | Owner-reported cost | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Front coil spacers + rear lift shackles | Spacer on top of the coil-over; longer rear shackles | about 2 in | about $300 for the cheap end | [S3][S4] |
| Spacer + add-a-leaf + upper arms | As above, with an add-a-leaf in the rear and aftermarket upper arms | about 2 in | about $650 as a kit (Nisstec, 2016) | [S6] |
| Coil-overs + upper arms + new rear leaf packs | Complete suspension, e.g. Old Man Emu (OME) or Ironman | 2-3 in | $1,000-1,500 | [S3][S6] |
| Titan-width front | Longer Titan CV axles and arms for 3-4 in | 3-4 in | about $2,500 and up | [S3] |
| Body lift | Spacers between frame and body | 2 in | | [S7][S8] |
| Drop-bracket lift | Lowers the front suspension mounts; usually with Titan knuckles and brakes, and often 18 in wheels | large | | [S9] |

A **body lift** makes room for bigger tires, but doesn't change suspension travel [S8]. One owner took 8-12 hours on a 2 in body lift, which included relocating the bumpers and fitting a longer radiator hose (NAPA 8978) [S7].

## Things that go wrong, and the fixes

- **Upper arm hits the coil bucket** ("CBC"). It's the classic clunk on drops after a front lift with stock upper arms, and it can crack the arm or the bucket. Fit aftermarket upper arms (SPC tube-style arms are popular, sold by PRG and as Mevotech), grind the bucket edge, or add bigger bump stops [S10].
- **Speed-specific vibration after a rear lift**, felt in the whole truck around 25-40 mph, is the **pinion angle**. Fit angled shims between the leaf and the axle perch with the thick end forward, and use new U-bolts. The rule of thumb is about 1° per inch of lift; measure the driveline angles to be sure [S11].
- **Front end link bind**: with more droop, the stock sway bar links can bottom out. Fit extended links [S10].
- **Cheap upper arm kits:** owners report poor hardware and ball joints that failed within a month [S6].
- **Shock cover adapter:** don't reuse the Off-Road/PRO-4X shock cover adapter with an aftermarket coil-over. It adds droop and can let the upper arm hit the bucket [S12].
- After any front lift, **get an alignment**. Lower arms with straight bolts need cam bolts before camber and caster can be set [S1].

## Installing front spacers or coil-overs

The front coil-over comes out without separating the upper ball joint, though many owners disconnect it for room. See [Shocks, springs and bump stops](shocks-springs.md#front-coil-over-removal-and-installation) for the full procedure and torques [S1][S12][S13].

Spacer kits differ. One installer found the Daystar kit went in differently from the Automotive Customizer kit: he had to disconnect the upper ball joint from the knuckle to get the strut out, while the ball joint itself stays on the upper arm [S13].
