# Front hubs, wheel bearings and CV axles

[Guides](index.md) · [Reference home](../index.md) · Related: [Brakes](brake-service.md), [Torsion bars](torsion-bars.md), [Transmission and transfer case](transmission-transfer-case-issues.md)

Each front wheel hub turns on an inner and an outer bearing, each with its own race, held by a lock nut on the knuckle spindle and adjusted for preload. On 4WD models the hub carries an auto-locking free-running hub [S1].

Nissan's steps and values below are from the 2004 manual. The earlier years' FAX sections haven't been compared with it, so check your own year's manual if it differs [S1].

**Grease:** multi-purpose NLGI No. 2 lithium grease for the bearings; Genuine Nissan grease or an equivalent for the free-running hubs [S2].

**Special tools:** wheel bearing lock nut wrench KV40105400 (J36001), for 4WD models only, and bearing race drift KV401021S0 [S1].

**How often:** Nissan's Schedule 1 is for hard use, such as short trips, dusty or rough roads, or towing [S2].
- **4WD:** it inspects the front wheel bearing grease and the free-running hub grease at 15,000 miles (24,000 km) or 12 months, and replaces them at 30,000 miles (48,000 km) or 24 months [S2].
- **Driving often in water:** replace that grease every 3,750 miles (6,000 km) or 3 months [S2].
- **2WD:** it inspects the bearing grease at 30,000 miles (48,000 km) or 24 months [S2].
- **Drive shaft boots:** they are inspected every 7,500 miles (12,000 km) or 6 months [S2].

## Checking for play

1. Shake each front wheel. If it is loose, adjust the wheel bearings first, then check the ball joints' end play [S1].
2. The bearings should turn smoothly with no axial end play. Adjust the preload if there is any end play or roughness [S1].
3. Make sure the cotter pins are in place, and that axle and suspension nuts and bolts are tight [S1][S2].
4. Check each drive shaft boot for cracks and grease leaks [S2].
   - Some wheel-side joints have a plastic boot instead of rubber. Don't take that kind of joint apart: if its boot or joint is damaged, replace the whole drive shaft [S1].

## Removing the hub and bearings

1. If the truck has ABS, unplug the wheel sensor and move it out of the way first, so its wires aren't damaged [S1].
2. **4WD:** remove the free-running hub. See [Auto-locking hubs](#auto-locking-hubs) [S1].
3. Unbolt the brake caliper and hang it with wire, leaving the hose connected and untwisted. Don't press the brake pedal while it's off, or the piston will come out [S1].
4. **4WD:** remove the lock washer [S1].
5. Remove the lock nut. On 4WD models this needs the lock nut wrench [S1].
6. Pull the hub off with its bearings, catching the outer bearing so it doesn't drop [S1].

## Inspection and reassembly

1. Clean the bearings and hub. The bearings must roll freely, with no noise, cracks, pitting or wear [S1].
2. Nissan checks the hub for cracks with a magnetic or dye penetrant test [S1].
3. To replace the races, drive the old races and the grease seal out with a brass bar. Then drive each new race in with the drift until it is fully seated [S1].
4. The ABS sensor rotor is always replaced with a new one, pressed on facing the direction the manual's drawing shows [S1].
5. **2WD:** pack the hub and hub cap with multi-purpose grease [S1].
6. Grease each bearing cone. Pack the new grease seal's lip with grease and drive the seal into the hub [S1].

## Adjusting preload: 2WD

1. Clean everything, then grease sparingly: the spindle threads, the face where the lock washer meets the outer bearing, the seal lip, and the hub cap [S1].
2. Tighten the lock nut to 34-39 N·m (25-29 ft-lb) [S1].
3. Turn the hub several times both ways to seat the bearings, then tighten the nut to the same torque again [S1].
4. Back the nut off 45° [S1].
5. Fit the adjusting cap and a new cotter pin. If the slot doesn't line up, loosen the nut no more than 15° [S1].
6. Check that there is no end play, and measure the preload at a wheel hub bolt. Repeat until it is in range [S1].
7. Spread the cotter pin and fit the hub cap [S1].

## Adjusting preload: 4WD

1. Clean everything, then grease sparingly: the spindle threads, the face where the washer meets the outer bearing, and the seal lip [S1].
2. With the lock nut wrench, tighten the nut to 78-98 N·m (58-72 ft-lb). Turn the hub several times both ways [S1].
3. Loosen the nut until it is at 0 N·m (0 ft-lb), then retighten it to 0.5-1.5 N·m (4.3-13.0 in-lb) [S1].
4. Turn the hub several times both ways, then retighten to 0.5-1.5 N·m (4.3-13.0 in-lb) again [S1].
5. Check for zero end play. Measure the force to start the hub turning at a wheel hub bolt: this is force A [S1].
6. Fit the lock washer, tightening the nut 15° to 30° to line up its screw holes [S1].
7. Turn the hub several times both ways, then measure the starting force again: this is force B [S1].
8. The preload is B minus A, and must be 7.06-20.99 N (1.59-4.72 lb) [S1].
   - If it's out, take the lock washer off and turn the nut up to 15° either way, then measure again. Repeat until the end play and preload are both right [S1].
9. Tighten the lock washer screws to 1.2-1.8 N·m (10.4-15.6 in-lb) [S1].
10. Fit the free-running hub and tighten its bolts to 25-34 N·m (18-25 ft-lb) [S1].

## Auto-locking hubs

**How they work:** the hubs lock when you shift the transfer case into 4WD and drive. To unlock them, shift to 2WD and back up in a straight line for at least 2-3 m (7-10 ft) [S1].

**Ratcheting noise** usually means one hub is locked and the other isn't. The noise comes from the side *opposite* the locked hub [S1].
- A noisy hub doesn't necessarily damage the good one, and the hubs don't have to be replaced in pairs: replace only the faulty one [S1].

| When the noise starts | Nissan's fix |
|---|---|
| After shifting into 4WD above 40 km/h (25 mph) | Slow down below 40 km/h (25 mph) or stop. Shift back to 2H, then to 4H, and drive forward until the hubs lock [S1]. |
| After shifting into 4WD below that speed | The shift didn't finish, so only one hub locked. Check that the 4WD lamp is on; slow or stop, shift to 2H and back to 4H, and drive forward [S1]. |
| After shifting into 4WD on snow, mud or a slope | The rear wheels slipped while the hubs locked. Drive forward slowly with less throttle until they lock evenly [S1]. |
| After shifting to 2WD and backing up | The hubs didn't fully release. Stop, check the lever is fully in 2H, and back up slowly and straight for at least 2-3 m (7-10 ft) [S1]. |
| In extreme cold | Thick differential oil can lock one hub. Drive 10 minutes or more in 4H to warm it, then shift to 2WD and back up straight for at least 2-3 m (7-10 ft). A thinner differential oil may be needed [S1]. |
| Continuously on one wheel, driving forward | A hub may be locked by damage or wrong assembly. Remove and inspect both, especially the one opposite the noise [S1]. |

**Removing and refitting a hub** [S1]:
1. Unbolt and remove the hub assembly [S1].
2. Remove the snap ring, then the spindle washer and the fixed cam assembly [S1].
3. Fit the fixed cam with its tabs in the knuckle's notches, greasing the parts the manual shows [S1].
4. Slide the spindle washer, then the snap ring, onto the axle shaft, between its two grooves [S1].
5. Support the axle shaft from behind the knuckle. Drive the snap ring into the inner groove with a deep socket, and check by eye that it is fully seated [S1].
6. Refit the hub and check that it works [S1].
   - Any hub, old or new, must slide on by hand and sit flush. If it doesn't, it is misaligned inside: take it off and turn it until it fits. Pulling it down with the bolts damages it [S1].

**Inspecting a hub** [S1]:
- Check the axle's end play [S1].
- A fixed cam (thrust washer) with galling or heat damage usually means too little axle end play. Replace it and recheck the end play [S1].
- Hold the hub's inner splines on a finger and spin the outer body. Replace a damaged hub, or one that clicks loudly as it spins [S1].
- New hubs come greased, need no more grease, and come with fixed cams [S1].

## Converting to manual hubs

These steps and notes are owners' experience; Nissan's manual doesn't cover manual hubs [S3].

1. Remove the auto hub's bolts with a 6 mm Allen key that fits tightly, so you don't strip them [S3]. The writer took the wheels off; another owner says you don't need to [S3].
2. Pull off the hub and remove the snap ring. Owners recommend snap ring pliers over screwdrivers [S3].
3. Remove the auto hub's inner parts [S3].
4. Test-fit the new hub on the axle splines. One owner says the hubs must be 28-spline [S3].
5. Grease the hub's inner parts, bolt the body on, then fit the dial cap. Set the hubs to FREE and tighten everything [S3].

**The snap ring:** the write-up leaves it off with the other auto hub parts, while another owner says it goes back in [S3]. In Nissan's manual, the snap ring on the end of the drive shaft sets the shaft's end play, and a removed snap ring isn't reused [S1]. Follow your hub kit's instructions.

**Keeping the bolts tight:** owners report hub bolts backing out, even with lock washers [S3].
- One owner uses cap screws with red thread locker. With blue thread locker, a couple came out on a Moab trail [S3].
- Another says thread locker doesn't hold well with the heat from the brakes; plain bolts with lock washers worked for him [S3].
- Warn hubs come with studs and nuts. Some owners replaced the studs with cap screws [S3].

**Which hubs:** owners rated the factory manual hubs from 1994-1997 Nissan Hardbody pickups highly. They are rare, and turn less than half a turn to engage [S3]. One owner said Warn hubs are good once the studs are replaced, and that Mile Marker hubs are known to fail [S3].

**Trade-offs:** with manual hubs you have to get out to lock them. In return, you know the hubs are locked, and you get 2WD low for backing a trailer [S3]. One owner found manual hubs loaded a worn CV joint harder, so it popped more often [S3].

## Replacing a CV axle (4WD)

**Nissan's removal** [S1]:
1. Remove the free-running hub and the snap ring [S1].
2. Remove the torsion bar. See [Torsion bars](torsion-bars.md) [S1].
3. Remove the shock absorber's lower bolt [S1].
4. Support the lower link with a jack and remove its bolts [S1].
5. Remove the drive shaft to final drive bolts [S1].
6. Tap the end of the drive shaft to free it from the knuckle [S1].

**Refitting:** grease the shaft and adjust the wheel bearing preload. Then set the drive shaft's end play to 0.10-0.45 mm (0.004-0.0177 in), measured with a dial gauge on the shaft end [S1]:
- start with a new snap ring as thick as the old one;
- swap to a thicker or thinner one until the end play is in range [S1].

Refit the torsion bar, then the free-running hub [S1].

**What owners add** [S4]:
- One owner's shortcut: you have to remove the tire, but not the upper control arm. Remove the upper ball joint's castle nut and the shock's lower mount instead [S4].
- **Stuck ball joint:**
  - a pickle fork can tear the boot [S4];
  - hammering the knuckle where the ball joint passes through can vibrate it loose [S4];
  - a propane torch freed one owner's in five minutes after an hour of hammering [S4];
  - with the nut off, driving the other side up onto something can pop the joint loose under the truck's weight [S4].
- **Dropping the front differential** instead: one owner did it in under an hour with a motorcycle jack. He removed the front cross member, the CV bolts at the differential, its six mounting bolts, the four driveshaft bolts and the breather clamp [S4].
- **Stock CV axles:** owners say they are strong enough with independent front suspension, cheap and quick to swap. Most failures start with a torn boot and dirty grease [S5].

## Clunk at full steering lock

Owners trace this to the steering stops more often than to the CV joints [S6].
- There are two stops on each side, on the lower control arms. They work in diagonal pairs: a turn is stopped by one stop on that side and the diagonally opposite stop on the other side [S6].
- Worn stops that have lost their caps clunk. Greasing the contact faces can quiet them [S6].
- For replacement stops, one owner says to order the Frontier 4x4 parts, listed under the Frontier and not the Xterra. The 2WD stops are too small [S6].
- Also check the ball joints, the front sway bar's bushings and its end links. One end link was broken but looked whole when parked, because the pieces rested together [S6].
