# Transmission, transfer case, clutch and differential fluids

[Guides](index.md) · [Reference home](../index.md) · Related: [Front hubs and CV axles](wheel-bearing-service.md), [Solid axle swap](solid-axle-swap.md)

Nissan's manuals list two manual gearboxes: the FS5W71C, in the KA24DE four-cylinder's fluid table, and the FS5R30A, in the V6's [S1][S2]. The V6 tables list the TX10A transfer case for 4WD [S1][S2].

This guide has Nissan's fluid checks, fluid changes and capacities, plus owners' experience with the clutch, the transfer case and gearbox swaps. It doesn't have Nissan's overhaul or removal steps: those are in the manual's MT, AT and TF sections, which haven't been read for this guide.

**Plug torques (2004 manual)** [S1]:

|---|---|---|---|
| Manual gearbox filler and drain plugs | 25-34 N·m (18-25 ft-lb) | Yes, anaerobic liquid gasket | [S1] |
| Automatic drain plug | 29-39 N·m (22-29 ft-lb) | Not stated | [S1] |

**How often:** Nissan's Schedule 1 is for hard use, such as short trips, dusty or rough roads, or towing [S1].
- It inspects the gearbox oil, automatic fluid, transfer fluid, differential oil and LSD oil every 15,000 miles (24,000 km) or 12 months [S1].
- It replaces the LSD oil at 30,000 miles (48,000 km) or 24 months [S1].
- **Towing, a car-top carrier, or rough or muddy roads:** change (not just inspect) these oils every 30,000 miles (48,000 km) or 24 months, and the LSD oil every 15,000 miles (24,000 km) or 12 months [S1].

## Manual gearbox oil

1. Check for leaks, then check the level at the filler plug. **Never start the engine while checking it** [S1].
2. To change it, drain at the drain plug, refill with new oil and recheck the level [S1].
3. Put anaerobic sealant on the plug threads and tighten both plugs to 25-34 N·m (18-25 ft-lb) [S1].

**4WD with a manual gearbox:** after driving in deep water or mud, pull the sealing grommet on the clutch housing and check for water inside [S1].

## Automatic fluid

**Checking** [S1]:
1. Warm up the engine and check for leaks [S1].
2. Before driving, with the fluid at 30-50 °C (86-122 °F), park level with the parking brake on [S1].
3. Start the engine and move the lever through every position, then leave it in P [S1].
4. With the engine idling, read the dipstick's COLD range. If the level is low, add fluid down the charging pipe, and don't overfill [S1].
5. Drive about 5 minutes in town, then recheck on the HOT range at 50-80 °C (122-176 °F) [S1].
6. Check the fluid's condition [S1]:
   - **Very dark or burnt-smelling:** the transmission needs diagnosis (the manual's AT section). Flush the cooling system after the repair [S1].
   - **Friction material in it:** after the repair, replace the radiator and flush the cooler lines with solvent and compressed air [S1].

**Changing** [S1]:
1. Warm up the fluid, then stop the engine [S1].
2. Drain at the drain plug and refill with the same volume that came out [S1].
3. Tighten the drain plug to 29-39 N·m (22-29 ft-lb) [S1].
4. Idle the engine for five minutes, then check the level and condition. If the fluid is still dirty, repeat the drain and refill [S1].

**What owners add:**
- **No pressure flush at home:** one owner does several drain-and-refills with driving in between. A shop flushes the whole system through the cooler lines behind the radiator [S5].
- **Old, dark fluid:** one owner warns a flush can cause problems in a transmission whose fluid is already worn out, and suggests a drain and refill instead [S5].
- **Filter:** one owner says the transmission has only a screen, not a real filter. He fitted an inline filter for about $30 [S5].
- **Slipping after bigger tires:** one 2004 slipped on the 1-2 and 2-3 shifts at low speed and part throttle after going from 265/70R16 to 265/75R16 tires [S6].
  - Disconnecting the battery only cured it for a few days [S6].
  - Owners thought it was the transmission, not the tires, and suggested a drain and refill [S6].
  - If that helps, drive a few miles and do another; if it doesn't, see a transmission shop [S6].
- **Supercharged and non-supercharged automatics** both fit, one owner says. Their input shafts differ only in material [S7].

## Transfer case (TX10A)

**Fluid:** Nissan fills it with ATF at the factory [S1].
- For a complete change, use Matic "D" or Canada Nissan ATF, or API GL-4 gear oil in SAE 75W-85 or 75W-90. It holds 2.2 L (2 3/8 US qt) [S1].
- Check the level at the filler plug, **never with the engine running** [S1].
- Put sealant on the plug threads and tighten both plugs to 25-34 N·m (18-25 ft-lb) [S1].

**Noises:** before rebuilding a case for a rumble from the rear output, one owner says to check the rear axle's backlash and pinion bearing, and the U-joints [S4]. He adds that slight play at the case's rear output is normal in his experience [S4].

**Stuck in 4WD:** having to back up a short way to get out of 4WD is how the auto-locking front hubs unlock [S15]. See [Auto-locking hubs](wheel-bearing-service.md#auto-locking-hubs) before blaming the case.

**Swapping in a Pathfinder case with a wider chain** [S3]:
- One owner says Pathfinders used the TX10 through 2001, and the TX14 from 2001 [S3].
- One owner who made the swap says:
  - any V6 TX10 bolts in [S3];
  - 1994 and later cases have an electronic speed sensor instead of a cable [S3];
  - the change to the narrower, weaker chain came in mid-2001 [S3].
  
  He suggests a 1996-2001 Pathfinder, a 1998-2000 Frontier or a 2000 Xterra case [S3].
- **Match the gearbox:** manual and automatic cases differ at the input, so match yours. The spline count also differs between four-cylinder and V6 cases [S3].
- **4WD indicator:** the Xterra case has a nub on a shift fork that presses the 4WD indicator switch. The Pathfinder case lacks it, and its switch sits elsewhere. One owner says you can split the case and swap the parts [S3].
- **Shift linkage:** the levers differ, so the swapper made a new shift linkage [S3].
- **Lower low range:** one owner points to Bandit4X4's exchange cases with lower low-range gears. Calmini lists lower low-range gears too, though he says they're rarely in stock; with those, low range is about 3.7:1 instead of 2:1 [S3].

**Shift knob removal** [S13]:
- The 4WD lever's knob screws on: M10 × 1.25, right-hand thread, with thread locker from the factory [S13].
- To get it off, take off the boot and clamp the shaft with locking pliers below where the boot covers. Wrap the knob in a towel and turn it hard with large pliers. Once the thread locker breaks, it spins off [S13].
  - Heat helps [S13].
  - Cutting the knob doesn't: the thread is in an insert inside it [S13].

## Clutch (manual gearbox)

- **Lifespan:** one owner read that the stock clutch only lasts about 70k miles. Owners reported 150k miles and 215,000 km on theirs, and one said it should last well over 100k [S9].
- **Upgrades:** one owner says clutch parts for the Z31 300ZX fit, because the pilot bearing, input shaft and flywheel diameter match [S9]. Check the fit before you buy.
- **Master and slave cylinders** [S10]:
  - If the engagement point wanders as the clutch warms up, replace both cylinders while you're in there [S10].
  - Both can be reached with the gearbox in or out [S10].
  - A leaking master cylinder shows as dampness at the pedal, under the floor mat [S10].
  - One owner found parts-store replacement cylinders kept failing [S10].
- **Crank position sensor code after a clutch job:** the sensor sits on top of the bellhousing and reads the flywheel teeth [S11].
  - One owner got a crankshaft position sensor code (he recalled P0335) right after a new clutch [S11].
  - Owners suspected the shop left it unplugged or damaged it while refitting the gearbox [S11].
- **Shift boot:** the inner boot that seals the gearbox's shifter opening is Nissan part 32862-3S500 [S12].
  - It stretches over the shifter if you lubricate the shaft and take your time [S12].
  - The manual shows special clamps for it; one owner used zip ties [S12].

## Swapping gears from a Pathfinder 5-speed

The early VG30 V6 4WD Pathfinder's 5-speed is also an FS5R30A and bolts in [S8]. One owner who did the swap posted the ratios:

| Gear | Xterra | Early Pathfinder | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st | 3.580 | 4.061 | [S8] |
| 2nd | 2.077 | 2.357 | [S8] |
| 3rd | 1.360 | 1.490 | [S8] |
| 4th | 1.000 | 1.000 | [S8] |
| 5th | 0.811 | 0.862 | [S8] |
| Reverse | 3.636 | 4.125 | [S8] |

His findings [S8]:
- **Differences:** the Pathfinder box differs mainly in its input reduction, with 5th gear one tooth deeper. Its breather elbow and hoses differ [S8].
- **Crank position sensor:** the Pathfinder box had no hole for it, so he drilled and machined the bellhousing [S8].
- **His results:** the used Pathfinder box he bought was worn out, though the lower gearing pulled better from a stop [S8].
  - He moved its 1st and 5th gears into his rebuilt Xterra box. That turns 2,100 rpm at 65 mph [S8].
  - Reaching 1st gear means stripping every gear and bearing off both shafts [S8].

## Differential oil

1. Remove the filler plug first, so you know you can refill before you drain [S14].
2. Drain at the bottom drain plug, refit it, and fill until oil runs out of the filler hole [S1][S14].
   - The front needs a pump to refill, because of where its filler plug is [S14].
3. Put sealant on the plug threads and tighten them to the torques above [S1].

**Is it a limited-slip?** Lift both rear wheels and turn one by hand. If both turn the same way, it is a limited-slip [S1]. One owner says there should also be a metal tag on the back of the differential [S14].

A limited-slip takes only a Nissan-approved LSD gear oil [S1]. One owner suggests adding friction modifier for a limited-slip [S14]; check that the oil you buy is approved for it.
