# Torsion bars: ride height, removal and adjustment

[Guides](index.md) · [Reference home](../index.md)

The first-generation Xterra's front suspension is an independent double wishbone with **torsion bar springs** instead of coil springs. Each torsion bar runs lengthwise from a torque arm at the lower link back to an anchor arm. An adjusting nut on the anchor arm bolt sets how much the bar is twisted, and with it the front ride height [S1].

**The left and right torsion bars are different.** Don't swap them [S1].

## Specifications

| Item | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Ride height "H" (unladen), 2WD | 37.7-41.7 mm (1.484-1.642 in) | [S1] |
| Ride height "H" (unladen), 4WD | 45.5-49.5 mm (1.791-1.949 in) | [S1] |
| Anchor bolt protrusion "L", standard | 68 mm (2.68 in) | [S1] |
| Anchor arm length "G", when fitting a new bar or anchor arm | 25-39 mm (0.98-1.54 in) | [S1] |
| Anchor arm lock nut | 30-40 N·m (22-30 ft-lb) | [S1] |
| Torque arm fasteners at the lower link | 45-60 N·m (33-44 ft-lb), 88-108 N·m (65-88 ft-lb) and 118-147 N·m (87-108 ft-lb), by position on the components drawing (p. SU-11) | [S1] |

**"Unladen"** in Nissan's manual means fuel, coolant and engine oil full, with the spare tire, jack, hand tools and mats in their places [S1].

**Measuring "H":** H = A − B, where [S1]:
- A is the height of the **center of the lower link spindle** above the ground;
- B is the height of the **bottom of the steering stopper bracket** above the ground.

Bounce the front of the truck 4 or 5 times first, so the suspension settles at its neutral height [S1].

## Rules for the adjusting nut

- **Adjust only by tightening.** Nissan says never to adjust ride height by loosening the anchor arm adjusting nut [S1].
- Before you remove the adjusting nut and lock nut, make sure the bar is no longer twisted, so it isn't holding load [S1].

## Removal

1. Move the dust cover aside [S1].
2. Paint matching marks on the torsion bar and the arms it fits into. **Use paint, not a scribe** [S1].
3. Measure how far the anchor bolt protrudes ("L"; standard 68 mm, 2.68 in) and write it down. Then remove the lock nut and adjusting nut [S1].
4. Remove the torque arm fixing nuts and pull the torsion bar forward out of the truck, together with the torque arm [S1].

## Inspect

- Torsion bar: wear, twist, bending or other damage [S1].
- The splines (serrations) on each part: cracks, wear or twist [S1].
- Dust cover: cracks [S1].

## Installation and adjustment

1. Grease the torsion bar's splines with multi-purpose grease [S1].
2. Raise the lower link until its bound bumper clearance "C" is **0**, that is, the bump stop just touches [S1].
3. Fit the torsion bar with its torque arm, the correct side's bar [S1].
4. Fit the anchor arm onto the bar, lining up the paint marks [S1].
   - **New bar or anchor arm:** set the anchor arm length "G" to 25-39 mm (0.98-1.54 in) instead [S1].
5. Tighten the adjusting nut until the anchor bolt protrudes by the "L" you measured at removal [S1].
   - **New bar or anchor arm:** use the standard 68 mm (2.68 in) [S1].
   
   Then tighten the lock nut to 30-40 N·m (22-30 ft-lb) [S1].
6. With the tires on the ground and the truck unladen, bounce the front to free up the suspension [S1].
7. Measure ride height "H" and compare it with the table above [S1].
8. If it's out of range, adjust the posture, then check the wheel alignment [S1].

Nissan's procedure ends with a wheel alignment check [S1].

**Lowering:** since Nissan allows adjustment only by tightening, our reading is that you back the nut off below the height you want and then tighten up to it [Unverified].

## How owners think about torsion bars

- **Cranking the adjusters changes ride height, not stiffness.** The bar holds up the same weight, so it ends up twisted by the same amount whatever the ride height. Two owners make this point [S2][S3].
- **Keep a bump stop gap.** One write-up says always leave at least 0.5 in between the upper control arm and the stock bump stop, even with aftermarket arms or low-profile bump stops. With no gap, the ride gets harsh and the front can't be aligned properly [S2].
- **Harsh ride after a lift?** Check tire pressure first. One owner's harsh ride after fitting 33 in tires was largely 50 psi in the tires: dropping to 40 psi and lowering the front 0.5 in made a noticeable difference. Another runs no more than 30 psi on 33s and sets it with a chalk test [S3].
- **Stiffer aftermarket bars** are for the weight of a steel bumper and winch, and help with body roll on a lifted truck. Owners in 2014 mentioned Calmini (20% stiffer) and Sway-A-Way bars from 4x4 Parts (30% stiffer) [S3][S4].
  - Stiffer bars need less twist, so the adjusters sit higher. One owner says the factory spec is a 40° adjuster angle when the front end is locked in; with Sway-A-Way bars and a 3 in lift his ended at 20° [S4].

## Re-indexing the bars

Re-indexing moves a torsion bar one or more splines in its anchor, so the adjuster has travel left at the ride height you want. One owner's write-up describes it [S2]:

- **When:**
  - the adjuster is screwed all the way up into the crossmember but you still have more than 0.5 in of bump stop gap and want more height [S2];
  - or the truck is at the height you want but the adjuster hangs below the crossmember.
- **Steps:**
  1. Set the tire pressures even and support the front of the truck under the differential with a floor jack, so the torsion bars carry no weight [S2].
  2. The adjuster bolt points up through the adjuster lever and the crossmember, about level with the rear of the front doors, with two nuts on top. Loosen the top nut with a 19 mm wrench and back the bolt out. Note where the adjuster lever sits relative to the crossmember [S2].
  3. Tap the adjuster lever rearward with a hammer to drive the bar forward out of the lower control arm's anchor. You may need to lower the jack a little first [S2].
  4. Turn the bar, typically 1-4 splines: away from the crossmember for more lift room, toward it to bring a hanging adjuster back inside [S2].
  5. Put anti-seize on the splines, refit the bar, and tap both ends to seat them [S2].
  6. Refit the bolt with anti-seize, set the ride height evenly side to side, and check the 0.5 in bump stop gap [S2].
  
  The writer measures ride height at the body seam below the doors, and likes a 1-1.5 in rake with the front lower [S2].
