# Timing belt (VG33E and VG33ER)

[Guides](index.md) · [Reference home](../index.md) · Related: [Engine oil change](engine-oil-change.md)

The VG33E V6 drives its camshafts with a toothed timing belt [S1][S2]. The four-cylinder KA24DE uses timing chains instead, and Nissan's timing belt interval does not apply to it [S1][S3].

| | |
|---|---|
| Applies to | 2000-2004 Xterra with the VG33E V6, and the supercharged VG33ER covered by the 2004 manual [S2][S4] |
| Interval | Replace every 105,000 miles (169,000 km), on both of Nissan's maintenance schedules [S3] |
| Difficulty | Hard [Unverified] |
| Time | A long day the first time [Unverified] |

**Do not let the camshafts and crankshaft turn separately with the belt off.** Nissan warns that the valves will hit the pistons [S1][S2].

## Specifications

| Item | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Camshaft sprocket bolts (both banks), lubricated with new engine oil | 78-88 N·m (58-65 ft-lb) | [S1][S2] |
| Belt tensioner nut | 43-58 N·m (32-43 ft-lb) | [S1][S2] |
| Crankshaft pulley bolt, lubricated with new engine oil | 191-211 N·m (141-156 ft-lb) | [S1][S2] |
| Upper and lower belt cover bolts | 3-5 N·m (26-43 in-lb) | [S1][S2] |
| Belt deflection, engine cold, midway between the camshaft sprockets | 13-15 mm (0.51-0.59 in) under 98 N (22 lb) of force, a reference value | [S1][S2] |
| Belt teeth | 133 total; 40 between the LH and RH camshaft marks; 43 between the LH camshaft mark and the crankshaft mark | [S1][S2] |

The tensioner stud takes locking sealant on its cylinder-block threads if it is removed [S1][S2].

## Before you start

- Work on a **cold** engine. Keep the belt, sprockets and tensioner clean and free of oil and water [S1][S2].
- Don't bend or twist the new belt [S1][S2].

## Removal

1. Remove the engine under cover [S1][S2].
2. Drain the coolant from the radiator, keeping it off the drive belts, and remove the radiator [S1][S2].
3. Remove the engine cooling fan and the water pump pulley [S1][S2].
4. Remove the drive belts: power steering, A/C compressor, alternator, and on the VG33ER the supercharger belt [S1][S2].
5. Remove all the spark plugs [S1][S2].
6. Remove the distributor protector, the A/C compressor belt idler bracket, the rocker cover's fresh-air intake tube, and the thermostat housing's water hose [S1][S2].
7. Turn the crankshaft to put No. 1 piston at **TDC on its compression stroke** [S1][S2].
8. Remove the crankshaft pulley bolt, pull the pulley off with a puller, and remove the upper and lower front belt covers [S1][S2].
9. Check the timing marks before the belt comes off [S1][S2]:
   1. the punchmark on the LH camshaft sprocket lines up with the punchmark on the rear belt cover [S1];
   2. the punchmark on the crankshaft sprocket lines up with the mark on the oil pump housing [S1].
   
   Thread the crankshaft pulley bolt back in temporarily so you can turn the crank later [S1][S2].
10. Loosen the tensioner nut, turn the tensioner off the belt, and remove the belt [S1][S2].

## Inspect

- **Belt:** Nissan replaces a belt with broken or cracked teeth, a cracked or worn back, worn or rounded edges, worn tooth faces, or oil or coolant on it [S1][S2]. It lists what each kind of wear points to:
  - broken teeth: a jammed camshaft or distributor, or a damaged oil seal;
  - a cracked back: a jammed tensioner, overheating, or the belt rubbing its cover;
  - worn teeth: a leaking water pump, poor cover sealing, or too much tension [S1][S2].
- **Tensioner and spring:** the tensioner must turn smoothly, and its spring must be in good condition. Replace the tensioner if not [S1][S2].

## Installation

1. Check that No. 1 piston is still at TDC on its compression stroke [S1][S2].
2. Fit the tensioner and its spring [S1][S2].
3. Turn the tensioner fully outward with a hex wrench and snug the lock nut temporarily [S1][S2].
4. Fit the belt, with the engine cold [S1][S2]:
   1. Line up the **white lines on the belt** with the punchmarks on both camshaft sprockets and the crankshaft sprocket [S1].
   2. Point the **arrow on the belt toward the front cover** [S1].
   3. Check the tooth counts: 40 teeth between the camshaft marks, and 43 between the LH camshaft mark and the crankshaft mark [S1].

## Set the tension (new or reused belt)

1. Loosen the tensioner lock nut. Turn the tensioner clockwise and counterclockwise with the hex wrench at least twice [S1][S2].
2. Tighten the lock nut to 43-58 N·m (32-43 ft-lb) [S1][S2].
3. Turn the crankshaft **clockwise** at least two full turns, then slowly bring No. 1 back to TDC on its compression stroke [S1][S2].
4. Push on the belt midway between the camshaft sprockets with 98 N (22 lb) of force. Deflection should be **13-15 mm (0.51-0.59 in)** with the engine cold [S1][S2].
5. If it isn't, go back to step 1 [S1][S2].
6. Refit the lower and upper covers at 3-5 N·m (26-43 in-lb), then the crankshaft pulley at 191-211 N·m (141-156 ft-lb), and the rest in the reverse order of removal [S1][S2].

**After a cylinder head or intake manifold job:** Nissan has a different tensioning procedure, with the rocker shaft bolts loosened and a 0.5 mm (0.0206 in) thick, 12.7 mm (0.500 in) wide feeler gauge run through the belt [S1][S2]. It's on pp. EM-79 to EM-80 of the 2000 manual [S1].

## Water pump

Nissan lists a leaking water pump as one cause of worn belt teeth and of coolant on the belt, and this job already removes the water pump pulley [S1][S2]. One owner says the pump is driven by the timing belt, so replacing it means taking off the crankshaft pulley and the whole timing cover, and the belt should be renewed at the same time [S5]. Nissan's water pump procedure is in the manual's cooling (LC/CO) section; the Internet Archive's copies of the 2000-2004 manuals that were checked do not include it [Unverified].

## What owners add

- **Do the related parts while it's apart.** One owner replaces the water pump, thermostat and camshaft seals along with the belt. He reported (in 2016) paying a dealer about $350 in parts and $700 in labor [S6].
- **Replace the tensioner too.** Another owner's tensioner failed on a trail: its bearings came loose inside the timing cover [S6].
- **Interval:** owners in one thread quote "100k" or "100k or seven years", a little sooner than Nissan's 105,000 miles [S3][S6].
- **Kits:** prices owners quoted in 2016 ranged from about $90 for a Gates kit to about $335 for a dealer kit [S6].
- **Working height:** one owner let most of the air out of the front tires to lower the truck, rather than stand on a stool. His harmonic balancer puller was too small, so he made an adapter for the end of the bolt [S7].
- **Was it done?** One owner read that replacement belts come with a sticker. Owners have found one on the door post by the passenger rear door, on the radiator support, or etched on a tab under the hood [S8].
