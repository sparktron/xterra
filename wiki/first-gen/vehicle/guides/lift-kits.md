# Lift kits, torsion bar lifts and body lifts

[Guides](index.md) · [Reference home](../index.md) · Related: [Torsion bars and ride height](torsion-bars.md), [Solid axle swap](solid-axle-swap.md)

The first-generation Xterra has torsion bars at the front and leaf springs at the rear [S1]. Owners lift it in layers:
- **front:** the torsion bar adjusters, usually with aftermarket upper control arms (UCAs);
- **rear:** an add-a-leaf or new springs, and lift shackles;
- **body:** a body lift of 1-3 in for tire clearance [S2][S3][S4].

## Before you buy a kit

- **Bouncy ride or topping out?** One owner suggests new shocks at all four corners and new torsion bars before a full kit. Buy long-travel shocks so they still suit a later lift [S4].
- **Torsion bar lift:** the bars set ride height, not stiffness. Keep at least 0.5 in between the UCA and the stock bump stop. See [Torsion bars](torsion-bars.md) [S6].
- **Upper control arms:** cranking the front up past what the stock arms allow needs aftermarket UCAs [S4][S6].
  - Owners in one thread rated Calmini's as heavily built, despite complaints about its customer service [S4].
  - Owners of another brand reported its upper ball joints wearing quickly. One said the arms take a mid-1990s Pathfinder ball joint, and found that replacement ball joints lasted [S4].
  - Owners advise against ball joint spacers, because they steepen the steering and CV angles [S4].
- **Rear:** owners mixed parts rather than buying a kit [S4]. One owner's list included:
  - Calmini UCAs;
  - an add-a-leaf;
  - revolver shackles;
  - extended rear brake lines;
  - camber bolts;
  - a U-bolt kit [S4].
  
  Stacking an add-a-leaf with lift shackles can over-lift the rear, so plan the total [S4].
- **33 in tires** need a 2 in body lift, some fender trimming, or both [S3][S4].

## Rules from Nissan's manual

- When rubber-bushed suspension parts are reinstalled, Nissan does the **final tightening unladen with the tires on the ground**. Unladen means fuel, coolant and oil full, with the spare, jack, tools and mats in place [S1].
- Each suspension fastener's torque is on the front and rear suspension component drawings (2002 manual, pp. SU-11 and SU-21 onward) [S1].
- After any suspension work, check the wheel alignment [S1].

## DIY 2 in body lift

One owner's write-up for 2000-2004 trucks puts it at medium difficulty, about $100 and 4-7 hours [S2]. A second owner's parts list built it for about $70-100 in 2011-2012, against $200 or more for a kit [S3].

**Parts** (from the parts list) [S3]:
- **Lift pucks:** 10 body lift pucks, 2 in tall by 3 in wide [S3].
- **Bolts into the body's welded nuts:** two M12 × 1.25 × 180 mm and two M10 × 1.25 × 160 mm, grade 10.9. These are the four mounts without a top nut [S3].
  - One owner could find the 180 mm bolts only from a specialty supplier; his 1 in lift needed only 160 mm [S3].
- **Bolts with nuts:** six 7/16-14 × 6 in grade 8 bolts with lock nuts and washers [S3].
- **Driveshaft safety loop:** two M10 × 1.25 × 80 mm bolts, plus spacers or washers if you keep the loop [S3].
- **Front bumper:** 2 in square steel tube to raise it [S2].

**Before lift day:** soak every body mount bolt and the steering shaft bolts with penetrating oil daily for a week or two. Four mount bolt heads inside the cab are under plastic caps and carpet [S2].

**Steps** [S2]:
1. Remove the front and rear bumpers. Unplug the fog lights and license plate lights first [S2].
2. Tie the steering wheel so it can't turn. **Loosen, don't remove,** the steering shaft clamp bolts so the shaft can extend [S2].
3. Free everything that would stretch [S2]:
   - unclip the heater hose clip in the engine bay;
   - remove the driveshaft safety loop's bolts;
   - slide the power steering reservoir out of its bracket;
   - loosen the intake hose clamps;
   - unbolt the brake line bracket on the passenger frame rail;
   - remove the lower half of the fan shroud.
4. Notch the floor for the transfer case shifter's 4LO travel if yours needs it [S2].
5. Remove the body mount bolts on one side and loosen them on the other [S2].
6. Jack **the body, not the frame,** slowly on wood blocks, listening for anything binding [S2].
7. Fit the pucks one at a time, with each bolt finger-tight and red thread locker [S2].
8. Repeat on the other side [S2].
9. Torque the bolts in a criss-cross order, not one side at a time. The writer's order is P1, D3, P4, D1, P3, D5, P2, D4, P5, D2, numbered front to rear, P = passenger, D = driver [S2].
   - His torques: P1/D1 23-32 ft-lb; P3/D3/P4/D4 22-31 ft-lb; P2/D2/P5/D5 14-18 ft-lb. These are one owner's figures, not checked against Nissan's manual [Unverified].
10. Raise the bumpers. The front sits on the 2 in square tube, drilled to match. For the rear, drill new holes 2 in lower [S2].
11. Refit the safety loop with the spacers, tighten the steering shaft bolts and intake clamps, and remount the power steering reservoir. One owner cut 2 in out of its bracket and welded it [S2].
12. Re-check every body mount bolt after 100 miles [S2].

**Gotchas owners hit:**
- Leaving the heater hose clipped can damage the heater core [S3].
- A heat shield raised with the body touched the passenger-side catalytic converter and rattled. Bending it away fixed it [S3].
- A battery cable fastened to the power steering reservoir bracket pulled on the battery terminal [S2].
- With a stock spare carrier, one owner had to lower the spare to let the rear of the body rise [S2].
- The heater hoses won't re-clip at the new height. Owners leave them unclipped [S2].
- A 3 in body lift is much more involved than 2 in. A 1 in lift avoids most relocation, but still needs the bumpers moved [S3].
