# Solid axle swap (SAS)

[Guides](index.md) · [Reference home](../index.md) · Related: [Torsion bars](torsion-bars.md), [Lift kits and body lifts](lift-kits.md)

A solid axle swap replaces the first-generation Xterra's independent front suspension, a double wishbone with torsion bars [S7], with a solid front axle, usually a Dana 44. It is a fabrication project: no part of it comes from Nissan, and everything below is owners' experience. One owner says to expect it to take twice as long as you planned [S1].

## Decide first: SAS or lockers?

- **Cost:** one owner put a complete SAS at $3,000 or more in 2013. A Calmini kit was about $1,500 then, plus springs or coils [S6].
- **For a daily driver,** owners in that thread steered a newcomer toward a rear locker first. A rear locker plus transfer case crawler gears gets you far before a SAS is worth it [S6].
- At the time, one owner knew of the ARB air locker as the only selectable locker for the first generation [S6].
- **A SAS improves articulation,** keeping the tires on uneven ground. It doesn't add traction in mud the way a locker does [S6].

## Axle choices

- **Common donors:** an early-1980s Jeep Grand Wagoneer Dana 44 (driver-side drop, without the vacuum disconnect), or a Ford Dana 44 from an F-150 or Bronco [S3][S4][S5].
- One shop owner uses Ford high-pinion Dana 44s and avoids the small Jeep axles [S5].
- **Avoid closed-knuckle axles,** one owner warns [S4].
- **Wheels:** the first-gen Xterra's bolt pattern is 6 on 5.5 in, like older Chevy trucks [S4][S5].
  - One owner fitted Chevy hubs and rotors to his Ford Dana 44 to get 6 lugs, which also narrowed it about 2 in [S4].
  - The stock alloy wheels may not clear Dana 44 brakes [S5].
- **Hubs:** many of these axles come without manual hubs, so add a hub kit. Keep the original slugs as a trail spare: if a hub breaks, a slug puts that wheel back in drive [S5].
- **Gearing:** a donor axle needs regearing to match the Nissan rear. One owner ran 5.13 gears with 35 in tires on a supercharged truck that came with 4.63s [S5].

## Suspension options

- **Spring-under-axle leaf springs:** one owner planned a Wagoneer Dana 44 with Rancho RS44044 leaves and a Bonney Motorsports kit [S1].
- **Coilovers with shock hoops,** on radius arms or links. Calmini sold a kit that it said lifts the truck about 6 in [S4][S5].
- **How high:** height comes from clearing the links and springs at full compression. Low builds exist; one owner's SAS on 37 in tires sat about 6 in up [S4].
- **Bump stops:** don't let the coilovers be your bump stops. One shop welds a flat pad to the axle and a tube with a poly bump stop to the frame, then sets the tube's angle with the truck on a ramp [S5].

## Driveshaft and transfer case

- The stock front driveshaft is too short for the droop, and binds, unless you run a high-pinion axle [S1].
- One owner fitted a Jeep XJ double-cardan front shaft with a flange and an adapter he made for the Nissan transfer case [S1].
- Another lists an adapter plate from the Nissan transfer case pattern to a standard Spicer pattern as needed for every SAS [S5].

## Steering

- Crossover steering kits are common. With high-steer arms, the drag link can run flat, for little bump steer [S1][S5].
- One shop uses a longer-than-stock pitman arm because of the Dana 44's steering arm length [S5].
- **Hydraulic assist:** owners say it's for rocks, and that the pump needs enough flow or highway steering feels sluggish [S5].

## Brakes after the swap

A pedal that goes to the floor is the usual complaint after a SAS [S1][S2].

- **Bigger master cylinder:** one owner bolted in a 1998-2001 Dodge Ram 1500 unit. The brake lines need metric bubble-to-inverted-flare adapters, or cut and re-flared lines. One owner had adapters leak and now always re-flares [S2].
- **Or remove the ABS unit:** several owners fixed the pedal by taking the ABS unit out and running hard lines from the master cylinder to the brakes, which let them keep the stock master cylinder [S2].
- **Bleeder screws:** on some Dana 44s the calipers fit either way round. Mount them with the bleeder on top [S2].
- **Bleed thoroughly:** owners said the air takes many rounds of bleeding to get out [S2].
- Extended brake lines are needed. One owner found a kit whose front lines fit the Chevy single-piston calipers on many Dana 44s [S1].

## Rear axle

The Nissan rear axle is strong, and owners keep it or swap to a Ford 9 in for cheaper lockers and gears [S5][S6]. A wider front axle needs rear wheel spacers to match track width [S4][S5].
