# Brakes: inspection limits, ABS codes and upgrades

[Guides](index.md) · [Reference home](../index.md) · Related: [Front hubs and wheel bearings](wheel-bearing-service.md), [Solid axle swap](solid-axle-swap.md)

The first-generation Xterra has disc brakes at the front and drums at the rear [S1][S2]. Owners describe the front calipers as two-piston [S3].

This guide has Nissan's inspection limits and checks, plus what owners report. It doesn't yet have Nissan's pad, caliper and shoe replacement steps or the caliper bolt torques. Those are in the manual's BR (Brake) section, which hasn't been read for this guide.

## Specifications

| Item | 2000 manual | 2004 manual |
|---|---|---|
| Front rotor thickness, standard / minimum | 28 mm (1.10 in) / 26 mm (1.02 in) [S2] | 28 mm (1.10 in) / 26 mm (1.024 in) [S1] |
| Front pad thickness, standard / minimum | 11 mm (0.43 in) / 2 mm (0.08 in) [S2] | 10 mm (0.39 in) / 2.0 mm (0.079 in) [S1] |
| Rear drum inner diameter, standard / repair limit | 295 mm (11.61 in) / 296.5 mm (11.67 in) [S2] | 295.0 mm (11.61 in) / 296.5 mm (11.67 in) [S1] |
| Rear shoe lining thickness, standard / minimum | 5.8 mm (0.23 in) / 1.5 mm (0.059 in) [S2] | 6.1 mm (0.240 in) / 1.5 mm (0.059 in) [S1] |
| Wheel nuts | 118-147 N·m (87-108 ft-lb) [S2] | 98-117 N·m (73-86 ft-lb) [S1] |
| Brake and clutch fluid | Genuine Nissan brake fluid or an equivalent DOT 3 (US FMVSS No. 116) [S2] | Genuine Nissan Super Heavy Duty brake fluid or an equivalent DOT 3 (US FMVSS No. 116) [S1] |

The wheel nut torque changed between these manuals, so use your own year's value. Both manuals say to retighten the wheel nuts once the truck has done 1,000 km (600 miles) after a tire rotation, and likewise after a flat tire [S1][S2].

**How often:** Nissan's Schedule 1 is for hard use, such as short trips, dusty or rough roads, or towing. It inspects the pads, rotors, drums and linings every 7,500 miles (12,000 km) or 6 months, and the brake lines and cables every 15,000 miles (24,000 km) or 12 months [S1].

## Nissan's checks

1. **Fluid:** the level must sit between the reservoir's MAX and MIN lines. A very low level means a leak somewhere in the system. If the brake warning lamp lights, check the fluid level switch and the parking brake switch [S1].
2. **Lines and hoses:** look for cracks, deterioration and damage. Then, with the engine running, press the pedal all the way down and check for leaks. Retighten a leaking joint, or replace the damaged part [S1].
3. **Front discs:** check each rotor's condition and thickness against the table, each caliper for leaks, and each pad for wear and damage [S1].
4. **Rear drums:** check each wheel cylinder for leaks, each drum's condition and inside diameter, and each lining for wear and damage [S1].

## What owners report

- **Rotor size:** owners in one thread posted front rotor diameters from 280 to 283 mm (about 11.1 in). Measure yours before buying a bigger caliper or rotor [S3].
- **Front pads wearing fast:** one owner's first check is the rear drums. Drums out of adjustment leave the fronts doing more of the stopping [S3].
- **Seized slide pins:** one owner at 200k miles found 3 of 4 caliper pins seized. He replaced the brackets, pins, boots and pads, using penetrating oil and a long breaker bar [S3].
- **Weak rear braking:** one owner found the fronts locked while the rears kept rolling. His possible causes were weight transfer, his armor, big tires and aging drum adjusters [S3].
- **ABS module or wheel speed sensor codes:** on one 2002 with codes C1111 and C1113, a damaged harness plug at the ABS module made several wheel sensors throw codes. Rodents had chewed the wiring [S6].
  - The repair used a used module and harness, new front speed sensors and a used G sensor [S6].
  - Another owner says swapping the module is easy if the brake line flare nuts come out cleanly. You can bleed the system yourself, but he still recommends a dealer brake fluid service [S6].
  - If a sensor works until you hit a bump, suspect the wire to that sensor [S6].

## Reading ABS blink codes

Owners posted the manual's self-diagnosis steps for these trucks [S5]. The owner who posted them says the system resets itself once the fault is repaired [S5].

1. Drive faster than 30 km/h (19 mph) for at least a minute, then turn the ignition off [S5].
2. Ground the data link connector's diagnosis terminal with a jumper wire [S5].
3. Turn the ignition on with the terminal still grounded and the brake pedal released. After 3 seconds the ABS lamp flashes the code [S5].
4. Look the code up in the manual's code chart and repair the fault, then erase the codes and re-run the self-diagnosis [S5].

The flashing stops after 5 minutes and starts again at the next ignition-on [S5].

**Which terminal:** owners disagree, and grounding the wrong one has blown fuses [S5].
- The posted procedure uses terminal 9. One owner says 2000 models use terminal 8 [S5].
- Two owners of 2000 trucks say terminal 8 blew the fuse for their gauges, and that terminal 9 works [S5].
- One owner blew a fuse on a 2000 using terminal 9 [S5].
- One 2004 owner shorted something while clearing codes and lost the gauge cluster and charging [S5].

Check your own year's wiring diagram before you ground anything.

Notes from the posted chart [S5]:
- Code 57 means low supply voltage, not a failed ABS unit [S5].
- Code 61 can mean a poor ground at the ABS motor [S5].
- A wheel that spins for 40 seconds or more on a slippery road lights the lamp without any fault [S5].

## Upgrades owners have made

- **Stainless steel hoses:** one kit covers all three hoses. Its longer rear hose is needed with a full 3 in suspension lift, and is a good idea with lift shackles [S3]. One owner says any brand installs the same way [S7].
- **Slotted front rotors:** they are side-specific, with separate left and right parts [S3].
- **Four-piston calipers from the 300ZX (Z32):** they fit the stock 283 mm rotor on a first-gen Xterra spindle, with a sleeve on each caliper bolt, because the spindle's bolt holes are one size larger [S3]. The owner who tried two versions of this on his 2000 Frontier got better stopping from the stock Xterra calipers, because the Z32 pads cover less of the rotor [S3].
- **Bigger master cylinder:** a 1999 Dodge Ram 1500 unit bolts to the stock mount. Owners quote its bore at 1 1/4 in, against 1 in for the stock one [S3].
  - It needs an M10 × 1.0 bubble-flare to M10 × 1.0 inverted-flare adapter on the front line, and a M12 × 1.0 bubble-flare to M10 × 1.0 inverted-flare adapter on the rear line. You can also cut and re-flare the lines [S3].
  - The same owner who tried the Z32 calipers got better braking from the 2003-2004 Xterra/Frontier master cylinder than from the earlier one [S3].
  - After a solid axle swap, see [Brakes after the swap](solid-axle-swap.md#brakes-after-the-swap).
- **Rear discs from a 1988-1995 Pathfinder:** see below [S4].

## Rear disc swap from a Pathfinder

The 1988-1995 4x4 SE Pathfinder had rear discs on an H233 axle, the same family as the Xterra's [S4]. The Pathfinder axle doesn't swap in whole: it is about 3 in narrower and has 31-spline shafts, against the Xterra's 33 [S4]. Instead, its backing plates, calipers and rotors move onto Xterra axle shafts [S4]. One owner estimated about 25% more stopping power afterwards [S4].

**Tools:** Nissan's axle tool J-45073, which removes the axle nut and pulls the bearing, and a shop press [S4]. The writer borrowed the tool from a dealer; another owner says it costs about $1,000 to buy [S4].

**Parts the writer used** [S4]:
- Pathfinder backing plates and calipers from a junkyard (calipers can be the core for remanufactured ones);
- Xterra axle shafts from a 4x4, because the 2WD setup differs [S4];
- new Xterra parking brake cables;
- new ABS tone rings, because the manual says to replace one whenever it is removed;
- four longer studs, Nissan part 43083-EB000, because the disc backing plate is thicker at the top two holes [S4];
- new outer axle bearings, grease seals, inner axle seals and lock washers;
- a 300ZX parking brake hardware kit (Carlson 17418). Its top return springs are too short, so keep the Pathfinder's [S4];
- Pathfinder rear rotors, pads and stainless hoses.

**Building each shaft** [S4]:
1. Flatten the lock washer's tabs, remove the axle nut with the special socket, and pull the shaft off its bearing with the tool [S4].
2. Drill and tap the backing plate (13/64 in drill, M6 × 1.0 tap) to take the Xterra parking brake cable's bolts. The short cable is the driver's side [S4].
3. Fit the longer studs and the parking brake shoes, press the new bearing race into the bearing cup, and fit the cup and a new seal into the plate [S4].
4. Press the shaft into the bearing. Fit the flat washer, a new lock washer and the nut, curved side down. The writer tightened the nut to 90 ft-lb [Unverified] and bent the lock tabs [S4].
5. Press the new ABS ring on, thin side toward the nut, until it touches the nut [S4].

The writer later had a bearing bind: he had partly pressed a shaft out to change studs, and the shaft had to come apart again [S4].

**Fitting it to the truck:** disconnect the parking brake cable, the hard line and the ABS sensor. Then remove the four nuts and pull the shaft. Fit a new inner seal, slide the disc shaft in and refit the four nuts. Reconnect everything, bend the hard line to meet the new hose, and bleed the brakes [S4]. Check that the hard line's flare nut matches the Pathfinder hose. If not, fit a Pathfinder flare nut and re-flare [S4].
